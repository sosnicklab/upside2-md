\title{A Table-Projected One-Particle dryMARTINI Membrane for Hybrid Upside Protein Simulations}
\author{}
\date{}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We present a one-particle representation of dryMARTINI DOPC for hybrid
protein--membrane simulations in Upside.  Each coarse-grained lipid (CGL)
carries a center, a head-to-tail orientation, and a scalar tail-compression
state.  Conservative CGL-CGL, side-chain--CGL (SC-CGL), backbone--CGL, and
environment interactions are projected from dryMARTINI constituent
interactions before simulation and stored as HDF5 spline tables.  A tempered
Boltzmann projection at 25 Upside temperature units prevents a small number of
low-energy conformers from dominating the reduced tables.  Pair-conditioned
tail relaxation supplies the steric relief required when two DOPC molecules
approach, while SC-CGL endpoint corrections are derived from the corresponding
single-lipid tail-relaxation problem.  No bilayer-thickness restraint,
orientational aligning field, force cap, or interaction exclusion is used.
The conservative model is separated from the dynamical mapping.  All Upside
and CGL forces are evaluated with one global step of
$0.004\,T_{\mathrm{up}}=40$ ps, protein carrier masses remain
$1\,m_{\mathrm{up}}$, and only CGL translation is calibrated with a mass
scale and a fluctuation--dissipation-consistent two-mode generalized Langevin
equation.  A 404-lipid membrane gives a coarse-graining-corrected long-lag
lateral diffusion coefficient of $11.45$--$11.47~\mu\mathrm{m}^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$,
consistent with DOPC measurements near 303 K.  Independent 1RKL and 1AFO
trajectories remain finite through four production segments, preserve protein
secondary structure, retain their initial protein--bilayer orientations, and
maintain closed, ordered bilayers.  The transport calibration leaves the
protein force field and protein time mapping unchanged.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The MARTINI family reduces molecular detail sufficiently to access membrane
length and time scales beyond routine atomistic simulation.
\cite{marrink2007}  Dry Martini removes explicit solvent while retaining a
coarse-grained lipid Hamiltonian suitable for membrane simulations.
\cite{arnarez2015}  Coupling that environment to Upside presents a distinct
multiscale problem.  The protein must continue to use the trained Upside force
field, whereas every protein--environment and environment--environment
interaction must remain active and traceable to dryMARTINI source physics.

The present model further maps all 14 dryMARTINI particles in one DOPC
molecule to one oriented CGL object.  A useful one-particle membrane must be
both thermodynamically coherent and dynamically interpretable.  An isotropic
center cannot encode the head--tail asymmetry of a phospholipid, and a rigid
oriented center cannot reproduce the relief obtained when resolved lipid tails
compress in a contact.  Conversely, fitting a membrane thickness or adding an
aligning field would make the force field depend on the desired bilayer rather
than on the represented molecule.

We therefore separate three questions.  First, conservative interactions are
projected from resolved dryMARTINI particles to CGL coordinates and tabulated.
Second, unresolved tail relaxation is represented by source-derived energy
differences and a scalar internal coordinate.  Third, translational transport
is calibrated independently with a CGL-only generalized Langevin equation
(GLE).  This division prevents a transport correction from changing protein
dynamics or compensating for an incorrect conservative potential.

The implementation follows the native Upside runtime contract: all
simulation-time conservative interactions are spline lookups from HDF5.  The
paper describes the force-field derivation, hybrid mapping, dynamical time
mapping, and validation tests in the same order in which they enter the model.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Source Hamiltonian, Units, and Runtime Decomposition}

All conservative membrane terms originate from the dryMARTINI topology and
nonbonded parameter files.  Source calculations use nm, kJ mol$^{-1}$, and
elementary charge.  Conversion is performed only when spline controls are
written to HDF5,
\begin{equation}
1~E_{\mathrm{up}}=2.914952774272~\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}},
\qquad 1~\mathrm{nm}=10~\angstrom .
\label{eq:units}
\end{equation}
For constituent particles $a$ and $b$, the nonbonded source energy is
\begin{equation}
u_{ab}(r)=4\epsilon_{ab}
\left[\left(\frac{\sigma_{ab}}{r}\right)^{12}
-\left(\frac{\sigma_{ab}}{r}\right)^6\right]
+\frac{k_e q_aq_b}{r},\qquad r\leq r_c,
\label{eq:source_pair}
\end{equation}
where $r_c=1.2$ nm and
$k_e=138.935458/15~\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}\,nm\,e^{-2}}$.  The denominator 15 is
the dryMARTINI dielectric constant.  A distance of $10^{-6}$ nm is used only
as a numerical singularity guard during CGL table construction.  It is not a
soft core or a fitted contact distance.  Bond, angle, and topology-excluded
intramolecular terms are read from the DOPC ITP file when isolated conformers
or tail-deformation energies are calculated.

The simulation potential can be written schematically as
\begin{align}
U={}&U_{\mathrm{Upside}}+U_{\mathrm{particle}}
+U_{\mathrm{SC-particle}}+U_{\mathrm{CGL-CGL}}
+U_{\mathrm{SC-CGL}} \notag\\
&+U_{\mathrm{BB-CGL}}+U_{\mathrm{CGL-target}}
+\sum_i F_{\mathrm{self}}(q_i).
\label{eq:runtime_sum}
\end{align}
Every term on the right-hand side remains active.  The first term is the
unchanged trained Upside protein force field.  The remaining conservative
terms are read from three artifacts: \texttt{particle.h5} contains generic
particle-pair tables, \texttt{sidechain.h5} contains rotamer-resolved
side-chain--particle tables, and \texttt{dopc.h5} contains the oriented CGL
tables and internal-state potential.  Preparation copies these data into the
runtime nodes \path{martini_potential},
\path{martini_sc_table_1body}, \path{cg_lipid_pair},
\path{cg_lipid_rotamer_sc}, and \path{cg_lipid_target}.  Consequently, no
Lennard-Jones, Coulomb, membrane-alignment, or protein--membrane term is
evaluated from an ad hoc analytic potential during dynamics.

For completeness, generic particle tables are generated directly from
Eq.~\ref{eq:source_pair}.  Unique $(\epsilon,\sigma,q_aq_b)$ triples are sampled
at 1000 radii from 0 to 12~\angstrom.  Only these generic radial grids replace
$r$ by $\max(r,0.1\sigma)$ at the first singular grid points; directional CGL
tables use the $10^{-6}$ nm numerical guard above.  The installed
\texttt{particle.h5} contains 81 such triples.

\subsection{Mapping a Resolved DOPC Molecule to One CGL}

Let $\mathbf x_k$ denote the 14 DOPC particle positions.  The CGL center
$\mathbf R$ is the geometric center of these positions, consistent with the
centering used when source conformers are generated.  The retained molecular
orientation is
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf n}=\frac{\frac12(\mathbf x_{\mathrm{C5A}}
+\mathbf x_{\mathrm{C5B}})-\mathbf x_{\mathrm{NC3}}}
{\left|\frac12(\mathbf x_{\mathrm{C5A}}
+\mathbf x_{\mathrm{C5B}})-\mathbf x_{\mathrm{NC3}}\right|}.
\label{eq:cgl_axis}
\end{equation}
Each reference conformer is centered and rotated so that
$\hat{\mathbf n}=\hat{\mathbf z}$.  A sampled CGL configuration with retained
axis $\hat{\mathbf n}$ and unresolved rotation $\phi$ is reconstructed as
\begin{equation}
\mathbf x_k(\mathbf R,\hat{\mathbf n},\phi,c)
=\mathbf R+\mathcal R_{z\rightarrow n}
\mathcal R_z(\phi)\mathbf x_k^{(c)},
\label{eq:reconstruction}
\end{equation}
where $c$ labels a stored conformer.  This reconstruction is used only to
construct tables; the 14 positions do not exist during CGL dynamics.

Geometric and inertial quantities are derived from the same constituent
coordinates.  For masses $m_k$ and axial projections
$z_k=(\mathbf x_k-\mathbf R)\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}$, the transverse inertia is
\begin{equation}
I_{\perp}=\sum_k m_k
\left(|\mathbf x_k-\mathbf R|^2-z_k^2\right).
\label{eq:inertia}
\end{equation}
The installed ensemble gives a center-to-tail projection of
$9.4059~\angstrom$, $I_{\perp}=2.2597\times10^4$
$\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}\,\angstrom^2}$, a maximum axial extent of
$18.1338~\angstrom$, and a maximum transverse extent of
$10.6122~\angstrom$.  These values determine the rotational dynamics and
neighbor-list support; they are not bilayer observables.

\subsection{Conformer Ensembles}

The CGL-CGL source ensemble is generated without a membrane or protein.  A
deterministic 14-particle reference is first embedded from the DOPC bond graph,
equilibrium bond lengths, and available equilibrium angles.  Isolated DOPC is
then sampled with the intramolecular Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}
U_{\mathrm{intra}}(\mathbf X)=U_{\mathrm{bond}}+U_{\mathrm{angle}}
+\sum_{a<b}^{\mathrm{nonexcluded}}u_{ab}(r_{ab}).
\label{eq:intra}
\end{equation}
A Monte Carlo trial chooses one particle uniformly, adds an isotropic Gaussian
displacement with standard deviation 0.025 nm, recenters the molecule, and is
accepted with probability
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{acc}}=\min\{1,\exp[-\Delta U_{\mathrm{intra}}/(k_BT_0)]\},
\qquad T_0=0.8647~T_{\mathrm{up}}.
\label{eq:mc}
\end{equation}
The installed CGL-CGL table embeds two references generated with seed 1729
and 2000 MC steps between stored references.  Exact source arrays are retained
in \texttt{dopc.h5}, making table regeneration independent of platform-level
differences in random-number generation.

Protein-interface tables require broader single-molecule geometries because a
contact can select conformations that are rare in isolated DOPC.  Their source
arrays are also embedded in \texttt{dopc.h5}: the SC-CGL base table uses eight
references, comprising resolved dryMARTINI conformers supplemented by four
isolated-DOPC representatives, and the CGL-particle table records twelve
resolved conformers selected from two full-resolution hybrid trajectories by
a local shell filter.  Only the internal coordinates of an individual DOPC
molecule are retained.  Protein identity, leaflet labels, bilayer normal,
leaflet separation, and any measured CGL distribution are absent from the
table coordinates and energies.  The independently generated 256-conformer
isolated ensemble used for the compression PMF is described below.

\subsection{Projection over Eliminated Coordinates}

At fixed retained coordinate $x$, the constituent energy is evaluated for
each discrete hidden sample $\omega$: conformer, axial bead-frame rotation,
azimuth, and, where applicable, rotamer.  Hidden samples are combined as a
tempered free energy,
\begin{equation}
F_{\tau}(x)=U_{\min}(x)-k_B\tau\log\left[
\frac{\sum_{\omega}w_{\omega}
\exp\{-[U(x,\omega)-U_{\min}(x)]/(k_B\tau)\}}
{\sum_{\omega}w_{\omega}}\right],
\label{eq:tempered_projection}
\end{equation}
where $U_{\min}$ is subtracted only for numerical stability.  Uniform hidden
coordinates have unit weights; Upside rotamers use their library
probabilities.  The installed directional tables use
$\tau=25~T_{\mathrm{up}}$, whereas the inverse table transform and production
dynamics use $T_0$.  The high projection temperature is therefore a controlled
soft minimum, not a simulation thermostat.  It retains the favorable trend of
many physically accessible hidden configurations without allowing a single
exceptional alignment to create a deep, localized attraction.  Direct energy
averaging was tested previously and produced CGL aggregation; the tempered
projection is retained for lateral homogeneity.

\subsection{Common Spline Representation}

All directional surfaces are represented with tensor products of cubic
B-splines.  For radial spacing $h$, radial and angular spline coordinates are
\begin{equation}
t_r=r/h,\qquad
t_a=1+\frac{N_a-3}{2}(a+1),\qquad -1\leq a\leq1.
\label{eq:spline_coordinates}
\end{equation}
The radial basis is clamped at the inner and outer boundaries and the angular
basis is clamped at $a=\pm1$.  Controls are obtained successively along each
tensor dimension.  In one dimension the fitted vector $\mathbf c$ solves
\begin{equation}
\left(\mathbf B^{\mathsf T}\mathbf B
+\lambda\mathbf D_2^{\mathsf T}\mathbf D_2\right)\mathbf c
=\mathbf B^{\mathsf T}\mathbf y,
\label{eq:spline_fit}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf B$ is exactly the basis evaluated by the C++ runtime and
$\mathbf D_2$ is the second-difference matrix.  Thus smoothing regularizes
curvature but introduces no membrane-specific target.  The production values
are $\lambda=0.1$ for CGL-CGL and SC-CGL, $0.01$ for CGL-particle, and $0.01$
for the scalar self PMF.  Fitted controls are clipped only to the range of the
sampled control values to prevent spline overshoot.

Directional free energies are stored through the invertible reduced-energy
transform
\begin{equation}
y(x)=\log\left[1+\frac{F_{\tau}(x)-F_{\min}}{k_BT_0}\right],
\qquad
F_{\tau}(x)=F_{\min}+k_BT_0[\exp y(x)-1].
\label{eq:table_transform}
\end{equation}
The reference $F_{\min}$ is stored separately for every table row.  This
transform compresses the repulsive dynamic range while remaining exactly
invertible.  It is not an energy cap.  Table support is calculated from the
largest constituent radius plus the 1.2-nm dryMARTINI cutoff.  In the last
radial interval the reconstructed energy is multiplied by the smoothstep
$s(u)=u^2(3-2u)$, with $u$ decreasing from one to zero at the cutoff; its
derivative is included in the force.

calculated, while ``controls'' are the dimensions of the runtime spline.

\subsection{CGL-CGL Constituent Projection}

For CGLs $i$ and $j$, define
\begin{equation}
\mathbf r_{ij}=\mathbf R_j-\mathbf R_i,\quad
a_i=-\hat{\mathbf n}_i\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij},\quad
a_j= \hat{\mathbf n}_j\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij}.
\label{eq:pair_coordinates}
\end{equation}
The base sample at $(r,a_i,a_j)$ is obtained by placing two reconstructed DOPC
molecules with centers separated along $\hat{\mathbf x}$.  Four equally spaced
azimuths generate each orientation satisfying $a_i$ or $a_j$; eight equally
spaced rotations about each molecular axis sample the discarded bead frame.
All four ordered pairs of the two reference conformers are included.  Each
retained point therefore projects 4096 constituent configurations before
Eq.~\ref{eq:tempered_projection} is applied.  The radial grid runs from 0.035
to 4.83 nm.  The resulting full tensor is symmetric under exchange of the two
identical CGLs.

The installed CGL-CGL surface contains no fluid-bilayer PMF, force-matching, or
iterative-Boltzmann-inversion contribution: the corresponding HDF5 counters
are all zero.  Its base energy is solely the sum of the $14\times14$
constituent interactions in Eq.~\ref{eq:source_pair}, followed by the physical
tail-relaxation correction below.

\subsection{Pair-Conditioned Tail Relaxation}

A rigid projection overestimates close tail-tail repulsion because resolved
DOPC tails can shorten and contract laterally.  Tail flexibility is integrated
out by comparing rigid and optimized energies at the same retained
$(r,a_i,a_j)$, not by fitting a bilayer potential.  The two tail chains contain
particles C1A--C5A and C1B--C5B.  For chain $c$, a weight $w_k$ rises linearly
from zero at C1 to one at the particle with the largest axial projection.  A
trial deformation is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf x_k'=\mathbf x_k-w_k\delta_c\hat{\mathbf n}
-w_k\rho\hat{\mathbf e}_{\perp,k},
\label{eq:tail_deformation}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_c$ is an independent axial shortening for each tail and $\rho$
is a common radial contraction for that lipid.  The radial unit vector is the
head-relative component perpendicular to $\hat{\mathbf n}$.  Each deformed
molecule is recentered, so Eq.~\ref{eq:tail_deformation} cannot change the
retained CGL center.

For two lipids, the effective energy minimized at a sampled hidden
configuration is
\begin{equation}
E_{12}(\boldsymbol\delta_1,\rho_1,
\boldsymbol\delta_2,\rho_2)
=U_{\mathrm{intra}}(\mathbf X_1')
+U_{\mathrm{intra}}(\mathbf X_2')
+\sum_{a\in1}\sum_{b\in2}u_{ab}(r_{ab}').
\label{eq:pair_relax_energy}
\end{equation}
The six variables are optimized by deterministic coordinate descent.  Axial
shortenings are bounded by 0.9 nm and radial contraction by the largest tail
radius of the corresponding reference.  At step sizes 0.20, 0.10, 0.05, and
0.025 nm, positive and negative moves of every coordinate are examined until
no move lowers Eq.~\ref{eq:pair_relax_energy}; the optimized value is never
allowed to exceed the rigid value.  Rigid and relaxed samples are projected
separately with Eq.~\ref{eq:tempered_projection}, and the correction is
\begin{equation}
\Delta F_{\mathrm{pair}}(r,a_i,a_j)=M(r,a_i,a_j)
\min[0,F_{\mathrm{rel}}-F_{\mathrm{rigid}}].
\label{eq:pair_relax}
\end{equation}
The binary support $M$ is one only for $r\leq2.5$ nm and
$a_i,a_j\leq-0.5$, the retained geometry in which the two tail faces oppose
one another.  The corrected energy grid is refitted through
Eqs.~\ref{eq:spline_fit} and \ref{eq:table_transform}.  Because the correction
is nonpositive and equals an explicitly paid intramolecular relaxation cost,
it can relieve source repulsion but cannot add an empirical attraction.  It is
baked into the base CGL-CGL tensor.  No pair-dependent compaction overlay is
applied during dynamics, avoiding double counting with the internal state.

\subsection{Internal Tail-Compression Coordinate}

Protein contact can select a single-lipid tail geometry without changing the
CGL center or orientation.  This response is represented by a scalar
coordinate derived from the head-to-tail extension,
\begin{equation}
Q(\mathbf X)=-10\left|\frac12(\mathbf x_{\mathrm{C5A}}
+\mathbf x_{\mathrm{C5B}})-\mathbf x_{\mathrm{NC3}}\right|,
\label{eq:source_q}
\end{equation}
where the factor 10 converts nm to \angstrom.  Larger $Q$ means a shorter
tail.  A dense isolated-DOPC simulation uses Eq.~\ref{eq:mc} with seed 1777,
20,000 burn-in moves, and 500 moves between each of 256 stored conformers.  The
lowest and highest quartiles define extended and compact pools; the two
conformers nearest each pool mean are retained as state representatives.  The
installed source centers are $Q_{\mathrm e}=-26.9632~\angstrom$ and
$Q_{\mathrm c}=-12.1348~\angstrom$.  Runtime coordinates use the affine map
\begin{equation}
q=(Q-Q_{\mathrm e})/(Q_{\mathrm c}-Q_{\mathrm e}),
\label{eq:q_map}
\end{equation}
so the extended and compact centers are 0 and 1.  A more compressed source
endpoint at $Q_{\mathrm x}=-9.0357~\angstrom$ maps to $q_{\mathrm x}=1.209$.

The self PMF is estimated directly from all 256 $q$ samples.  Twelve equally
spaced bins span the observed range plus a margin based on the 5th--95th
percentiles.  With the Jeffreys pseudocount $1/2$,
\begin{equation}
F_{\mathrm{self}}(q_b)=-k_BT_0\log
\frac{n_b+1/2}{\sum_{b'}(n_{b'}+1/2)},
\label{eq:self_pmf}
\end{equation}
and the minimum is shifted to zero.  A 14-control cubic spline represents
Eq.~\ref{eq:self_pmf} over $-0.3618\leq q\leq1.209$.  Its curvature gives the
state mass $17.670~m_{\mathrm{up}}$; the scalar thermostat time is
$1.0~T_{\mathrm{up}}$.  The state is propagated and checkpointed for every
CGL.  It contains no leaflet, membrane-plane, or protein-orientation variable.

\subsection{SC-CGL Base Energy and Tail-Relief Endpoints}

Upside side chains are represented by rotamer placement beads.  Each placement
bead carries its corresponding dryMARTINI type, and the CGL table is generated
for the 29 distinct residue/bead rows.  For a point side-chain bead $A$ at the
origin, a reconstructed CGL is placed at $r\hat{\mathbf x}$.  Its orientation
samples the coordinate $a=\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\hat{\mathbf x}$ with four
azimuths, eight bead-frame rotations, and the eight embedded DOPC conformers.
The constituent base energy is
\begin{equation}
F_A^0(r,a)=\mathcal P_{\tau}
\left[\sum_{k\in\mathrm{DOPC}}u_{kA}
\left(|\mathbf x_k-r\hat{\mathbf x}|\right)\right],
\label{eq:sc_base}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal P_{\tau}$ denotes Eq.~\ref{eq:tempered_projection}.  The
one-angular-dimensional result is stored in the runtime full-tensor layout by
replication over the point bead's unused orientation axis.  There is no fitted
isotropic background or excluded-area replacement.

The $q$ dependence is derived from a second, source-conditioned optimization.
At each $(r,a)$, the rigid objective contains the full DOPC intramolecular
energy but only the positive part of each tail-bead--side-chain contact,
\begin{equation}
E_A^+(\boldsymbol\delta,\rho)=U_{\mathrm{intra}}(\mathbf X')
+\sum_{k\in\mathrm{tails}}\max[0,u_{kA}(r_{kA}')].
\label{eq:sc_relax_energy}
\end{equation}
Using only the positive contact part is essential: favorable SC-lipid
interactions already reside in Eq.~\ref{eq:sc_base} and must not become more
attractive merely because $q$ changes.  The two axial shortenings and one
radial contraction are optimized with the same coordinate-descent schedule as
Eq.~\ref{eq:pair_relax_energy}.  Maximum shortening is 0.7444 nm at the compact
endpoint and 0.9 nm at the compressed endpoint.  After separate tempered
projection of rigid and relaxed samples,
\begin{equation}
\Delta F_A^{(s)}(r,a)=
\min[0,F_{A,\mathrm{rel}}^{(s)}-F_{A,\mathrm{rigid}}^{(s)}],
\qquad s\in\{\mathrm c,\mathrm x\}.
\label{eq:sc_relax}
\end{equation}
Relief is applied only where the base energy is positive, and the corrected
endpoint is floored at zero.  Attractive bins remain exactly equal to the
base table.  The endpoint energies are transformed with
Eq.~\ref{eq:table_transform}; differences from the base control tensor,
$\Delta y_A^{(s)}$, are then fitted with the same $25\times11\times11$ spline
basis.

Runtime interpolation is performed in control space,
\begin{equation}
y_A(r,a,q)=y_A^0(r,a)+w_{\mathrm e}(q)\Delta y_A^{(\mathrm e)}
+w_{\mathrm c}(q)\Delta y_A^{(\mathrm c)}
+w_{\mathrm x}(q)\Delta y_A^{(\mathrm x)}.
\label{eq:sc_mix}
\end{equation}
The extended correction is zero.  Between $q_{\mathrm e}=0$ and
$q_{\mathrm c}=1$, $(w_{\mathrm e},w_{\mathrm c},w_{\mathrm x})=(1-q,q,0)$;
between 1 and 1.209, the compact and compressed weights interpolate linearly.
Outside the endpoint interval the nearest endpoint is used.  Analytic
derivatives of the weights contribute to the force on $q$, so a protein
contact can physically drive compression rather than merely select a static
table.

\subsection{CGL-Particle and Rotamer-Resolved Protein Tables}

Backbone proxies and other dryMARTINI target particles interact with CGLs
through a separate direct table.  For target type $A$,
\begin{equation}
F_A^{\mathrm{target}}(r,a)=\mathcal P_{\tau}
\left[\sum_{k\in\mathrm{DOPC}}u_{kA}(r_{kA})\right],
\qquad a=-\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}_{\mathrm{CGL}\rightarrow A}.
\label{eq:target_table}
\end{equation}
The installed artifact stores this direct base tensor together with
single-CGL endpoint corrections for the extended, compact, and compressed tail
states.  Protein-facing target nodes therefore use
\begin{equation}
y_A^{\mathrm{target}}(r,a,q)=y_A^{0}(r,a)
+w_{\mathrm e}(q)\Delta y_A^{(\mathrm e)}
+w_{\mathrm c}(q)\Delta y_A^{(\mathrm c)}
+w_{\mathrm x}(q)\Delta y_A^{(\mathrm x)},
\label{eq:target_q_table}
\end{equation}
with the same piecewise-linear weights as Eq.~\ref{eq:sc_mix}.  Non-protein
targets continue to use the base tensor through \texttt{cg\_lipid\_target\_base}.
This is a model boundary rather than an interaction exclusion: BB-CGL and
CGL-target forces remain active, but protein overlap can now relax the same
hidden tail-compression coordinate used by SC-CGL.

For non-CGL environment particles, \texttt{sidechain.h5} is constructed from
the Upside orientation library and MARTINI side-chain geometry.  A canonical
C$\beta$ anchor at $(0,0.943756,1.206801)~\angstrom$ defines the side-chain
axis.  For each residue rotamer, all constituent side-chain beads interact
with a target placed at 96 radii from 0.25 to 1.20 nm and 24 Fibonacci-sphere
directions.  Samples are deposited linearly onto 13 cosine bins and projected
with Eq.~\ref{eq:tempered_projection}, using the rotamer probabilities as
weights.  Full rotamer grids, rather than a residue-averaged potential, enter
the Upside rotamer solver.  This preserves side-chain specificity in both
SC-particle and SC-CGL coupling.

\subsection{Runtime Energies, Forces, and Torques}

At runtime, a tensor lookup returns $y$, $y_r$, and angular derivatives.  The
inverse transform gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial F}{\partial \xi}=k_BT_0\exp(y)
\frac{\partial y}{\partial \xi},
\label{eq:inverse_derivative}
\end{equation}
before the cutoff taper and its derivative are applied.  For the CGL-CGL
coordinates in Eq.~\ref{eq:pair_coordinates}, the gradient with respect to
$\mathbf r_{ij}$ is
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial F}{\partial\mathbf r_{ij}}={}&F_r\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij}
-\frac{F_{a_i}}{r_{ij}}
(\hat{\mathbf n}_i+a_i\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij}) \notag\\
&+\frac{F_{a_j}}{r_{ij}}
(\hat{\mathbf n}_j-a_j\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij}),
\label{eq:pair_gradient}
\end{align}
and the orientation gradients are
$\partial F/\partial\hat{\mathbf n}_i=-F_{a_i}\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij}$ and
$\partial F/\partial\hat{\mathbf n}_j=F_{a_j}\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij}$.  Equal and
opposite center forces are accumulated.  The physical orientation torque is
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol\tau_i=-\hat{\mathbf n}_i\times
\frac{\partial F}{\partial\hat{\mathbf n}_i}.
\label{eq:orientation_torque}
\end{equation}
The orientation angular momentum is projected into the tangent plane of the
unit sphere, advanced by Eq.~\ref{eq:orientation_torque}, and the unit vector
is rotated with Rodrigues' formula.  The source-derived inertia in
Eq.~\ref{eq:inertia} is used, with rotational thermostat time
$0.008~T_{\mathrm{up}}$.  There is no aligning torque toward a bilayer normal.

SC-CGL derivatives are accumulated first on each placement bead and then
passed through the existing rotamer-energy derivatives.  Backbone-proxy
forces from Eq.~\ref{eq:target_table} are propagated to the Upside carrier
atoms through the stored virtual-site mapping.  The derivative of
Eq.~\ref{eq:sc_mix} is accumulated on the corresponding $q_i$ together with
$F'_{\mathrm{self}}(q_i)$.  Thus center translation, molecular rotation,
rotamer selection, backbone motion, and tail compression all receive analytic
forces from the same tabulated energy.

\subsection{Hybrid Protein Mapping}

The protein is not converted to a dryMARTINI protein.  Upside retains its
backbone coordinates, hydrogen-bond and secondary-structure terms, side-chain
rotamer solver, thermostat, and trained force field.  For each complete
residue, preparation records the Upside carrier atoms and a virtual
dryMARTINI-like backbone proxy.  Side-chain--environment energies enter the
rotamer solver through \texttt{martini\_sc\_table\_1body} and
\texttt{cg\_lipid\_rotamer\_sc}; backbone and generic environment interactions
enter through \texttt{martini\_potential} and \texttt{cg\_lipid\_target}.

Protein carrier masses remain exactly $1~m_{\mathrm{up}}$.  Neither the
protein masses nor the protein thermostat is rescaled to reproduce lipid
transport.  Although side-chain optimization may use its own internal
substeps, CGL-CGL, SC-CGL, BB-CGL, and protein forces are evaluated in every
global integration stage.

\subsection{Global Time Step and CGL-Only GLE}

One standard velocity-Verlet stage advances the full system by
\begin{equation}
\Delta t=0.004~T_{\mathrm{up}}=40~\mathrm{ps}.
\label{eq:timestep}
\end{equation}
There is no multiplier between requested steps and integrated steps.  Thus
$1~T_{\mathrm{up}}=10$ ns, and CGL-CGL, SC-CGL, backbone--CGL, and protein
forces all act on the same physical clock.

The native DOPC mass is $1008~\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}=84~m_{\mathrm{up}}$.  The
calibrated translational mass scale is 0.05, giving
$m_{\mathrm{CGL}}=4.2~m_{\mathrm{up}}$.  A two-mode Markovian embedding of an
exponential-memory GLE replaces the ordinary translational thermostat only for
CGL particles.  At $T_0$, the memory times and couplings are
\begin{equation}
(\tau_1,\tau_2)=(0.2,2.0)~T_{\mathrm{up}},\qquad
(g_1,g_2)=(0.30375,0.2205).
\label{eq:gle}
\end{equation}
For each Cartesian component, physical momentum $p$ and auxiliary momenta
$s_m$ obey the linear thermostat process
\begin{align}
dp &=-\sum_m g_m s_m\,dt,\notag\\
ds_m &=\left(g_mp-\tau_m^{-1}s_m\right)dt
+\sqrt{2m_{\mathrm{CGL}}k_BT_0/\tau_m}\,dW_m.
\label{eq:gle_sde}
\end{align}
The conservative velocity-Verlet kick is separate from
Eq.~\ref{eq:gle_sde}.  The coupled linear process is propagated exactly over
each global step: a matrix exponential gives the deterministic propagator and
a Van Loan block exponential gives the finite-step covariance, whose Cholesky
factor generates fluctuation--dissipation-consistent noise.  Auxiliary
momenta are stored in every checkpoint.  None of these operations acts on
protein carriers, orientation, or $q$.  Colored-noise embeddings of this form
separate the conservative free-energy surface from dynamical friction.
\cite{ceriotti2010}

\subsection{Diffusion Mapping and Analysis}

Lateral positions are unwrapped across the periodic cell.  At every frame the
instantaneous center-of-mass displacement of the CGL population is removed.
For lag $t$, the raw two-dimensional diffusion estimate follows
\begin{equation}
D_{\mathrm{CGL}}=\frac{1}{4}\frac{d}{dt}
\left\langle|\mathbf R_{xy}(t_0+t)-\mathbf R_{xy}(t_0)|^2\right\rangle_{t_0,i}.
\label{eq:diffusion}
\end{equation}
The model uses the prescribed temporal coarse-graining correction
\begin{equation}
D_{\mathrm{DOPC}}=(14\times4)D_{\mathrm{CGL}}=56D_{\mathrm{CGL}},
\label{eq:cg_factor}
\end{equation}
where 14 is the number of dryMARTINI particles represented by one CGL and 4 is
the MARTINI particle coarse-graining factor.  Because
$1~\angstrom^2/T_{\mathrm{up}}=1~\mu\mathrm{m}^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ under
Eq.~\ref{eq:timestep}, the numerical conversion is direct after multiplication
by 56.  The factor modifies the interpreted diffusion coefficient, not the
Upside simulation clock.

Bilayer validation also measures the mean leaflet-center separation,
$|n_z|$, lateral occupancy, low-wavevector density modes, nearest-neighbor
spacing, leaflet reassignment, and finite coordinates and energies.  Hybrid
validation additionally measures hydrogen-bond score, C$\alpha$ RMSD after
rigid alignment, DSSP agreement, helix fraction, and the angle between each
protein transmembrane axis and the bilayer plane.  No protein-angle restraint
is present.

\subsection{Simulation Protocol}

The transport calibration used a fresh 404-CGL DOPC bilayer at
$T=0.8647~T_{\mathrm{up}}\simeq303$ K.  The run contained 30,000 global steps,
or $120~T_{\mathrm{up}}=1.2~\mu$s.  Structural and multi-origin diffusion
statistics discarded the initial 20\%, leaving $96~T_{\mathrm{up}}$.

Hybrid validation used fresh 1RKL and 1AFO preparations with 275 and 174 CGL
particles, respectively.  Each system was propagated through stage 7.0 and
three sequential production continuations.  Every segment contained 2000
steps ($8~T_{\mathrm{up}}=80$ ns), for a total of 320 ns per protein.  The
workflow minimizes each continuation input before dynamics;
trajectory statistics are evaluated within segments so that minimization
displacements are not interpreted as diffusion.  CGL orientation, compaction,
GLE auxiliary momenta, and ordinary momenta are restored from the preceding
checkpoint.  Production burn-in restraints and protein orientation restraints
are zero.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Bilayer Structure and Lateral Transport}

The 404-CGL membrane remained closed and laterally uniform.  After the initial
relaxation, the mean leaflet-center separation was $16.52~\angstrom$ and the
final separation was $16.41~\angstrom$.  The mean orientational order was
$\langle|n_z|\rangle=0.9924$, with fifth percentile 0.9770.  Six-by-six
occupancy grids had no empty cells in either leaflet; low-wavevector density
amplitudes averaged 0.0158 and 0.0156 for the two leaflets.  The maximum
leaflet reassignment fraction was 0.00495 and did not grow into mixing or
ejection.

Table~\ref{tab:diffusion} reports the diffusion estimates.  The short-lag
window is subdiffusive ($\alpha=0.657$ in
$\mathrm{MSD}\propto t^{\alpha}$) and gives the largest value.  At longer
lags, the estimates converge: the 10--40 and 20--60 $T_{\mathrm{up}}$ windows
give $11.47$ and $11.45~\mu\mathrm{m}^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ after the factor of
56.  Lipid14 reports DOPC values of 6.48 and
$9.49~\mu\mathrm{m}^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ in NPT and NVE simulations at 303 K
and cites an experimental value of $11.5~\mu\mathrm{m}^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ at
303 K.\cite{dickson2014}  An independent DOPC study reports approximately
$9.32~\mu\mathrm{m}^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ at 298 K.\cite{ermilova2020}  The
long-lag CGL result therefore closes the intended DOPC time scale without a
change to the Upside clock.

\subsection{Clock and Restart Verification}

Every 2000-step hybrid segment records exactly $8.0~T_{\mathrm{up}}$, and the
30,000-step membrane trajectory records exactly $120~T_{\mathrm{up}}$.  The
prepared hybrid files contain protein carrier mass 1, CGL mass 4.2, the two
GLE modes in Eq.~\ref{eq:gle}, and all required conservative interaction
nodes.  CGL orientation and compaction restart errors are at single-precision
roundoff or zero.  These checks confirm the one-to-one mapping between requested
and integrated steps and show that transport calibration does not slow the
Upside protein core.

\section{Discussion}

The principal modeling result is the separation of conservative reduction from
dynamical coarse graining.  The tempered constituent projection and physical
tail-relaxation energies determine membrane structure and protein contact
forces.  The CGL mass and GLE determine how rapidly that conservative surface
is explored.  Changing the protein mass, changing the global time unit, or
weakening SC-CGL interactions would mix those roles and would not constitute a
physical transport calibration.

The tail treatment also avoids membrane-specific fitting.  Pair relaxation is
calculated from the energetic benefit and molecular cost of deforming two DOPC
tails at a specified pair geometry.  The SC-CGL correction is calculated from
the corresponding side-chain-conditioned tail relief.  The scalar state has an
isolated-DOPC self PMF.  None of these terms contains a target leaflet
separation or protein angle.  Bilayer closure and protein orientation are
therefore validation observables rather than force-field inputs.

The long-lag diffusion plateau is the appropriate clock diagnostic.  Short-lag
motion is subdiffusive because a CGL remains caged by neighboring lipids; in
protein-containing systems, crowding and direct contacts add further
subdiffusion.  That local slowing does not imply a different timestep for
SC-CGL interactions.  All conservative forces are evaluated at the same
40-ps global stage, while the protein-free membrane supplies the transferable
bulk transport calibration.

The present validation is limited to DOPC, 303 K, and two membrane proteins.
Other lipid chemistries require new constituent projections and source-derived
internal relaxation surfaces.  Quantitative hydrodynamic finite-size analysis
and a larger protein panel would be appropriate before using the model for
absolute kinetic predictions outside this regime.

\section{Conclusions}

The dryMARTINI--Upside interface separates conservative coarse graining from
dynamical calibration.  It uses source-projected spline tables for every
conservative interaction, retains the clustering-resistant tempered Boltzmann
average, represents unresolved DOPC tail relaxation in both CGL-CGL and SC-CGL
contacts, and keeps generic target interactions active.
Transport is corrected only on CGL particles with a mass scale and an
FDT-consistent GLE.  The Upside backbone remains on its original mass, force
field, thermostat, and 40-ps global integration step.

The 404-lipid membrane reproduces the experimental-scale DOPC diffusion rate
while remaining closed, ordered, and laterally uniform.  Fresh 1RKL and 1AFO
trajectories remain finite through stage 7.3, preserve secondary structure,
and fluctuate around their initial bilayer orientations without restraints.
Together these tests establish that the CGL time mapping reproduces
experimental-scale membrane transport without degrading protein dynamics.
