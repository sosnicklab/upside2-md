\section{Single-Particle Lipid Model for Hybrid Simulations}

\subsection{Coarse Lipid Coordinates}

We represent each dry-MARTINI DOPC molecule by one oriented coarse lipid
particle, denoted CGL.  The coarse position $\mathbf{R}$ is the geometric
center of the 14 dry-MARTINI DOPC beads, and the coarse direction
$\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ is the unit vector from the NC3 head bead to the midpoint of
the terminal tail beads C5A and C5B.  The resolved DOPC bead coordinates used
below are first centered at $\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{0}$ and rotated so that this
head-to-tail axis is the canonical lipid axis.

Upside stores oriented particles through ordinary Cartesian sites.  The CGL
orientation is therefore carried by a companion site, CGLD, at
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{CGLD}}=\mathbf{R}+\ell\hat{\mathbf{n}},
\end{equation}
connected to CGL by a harmonic bond,
\begin{equation}
  U_{\mathrm{CGL-CGLD}} =
  \frac{1}{2}k_{\mathrm{CGLD}}
  \left(|\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{CGLD}}-\mathbf{R}|-\ell\right)^2 .
\end{equation}
CGLD is a numerical orientation carrier, not a dry-MARTINI bead.  It has no
nonbonded interactions and no separate orientation potential.  All lipid
orientation preferences in the simulation come from the two-body spline
interactions described below.

The carrier parameters are derived from the same canonical DOPC geometry and
dry-MARTINI bonded topology used to build the nonbonded tables.  If
$\mathbf{x}_i$ and $m_i$ are the centered DOPC bead positions and masses,
\begin{align}
  \ell &= \left|\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{tail}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}\right|,\\
  I_\perp &= \sum_i m_i
  \left(|\mathbf{x}_i|^2-(\mathbf{x}_i\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}})^2\right),\\
  M_{\mathrm{CGLD}} &= I_\perp/\ell^2 .
\end{align}
The projected dry-MARTINI bonded stiffness along the CGL axis is retained as
metadata,
\begin{equation}
  k_{\mathrm{proj}} =
  \frac{1}{\kappa_E\kappa_L^2}
  \sum_{(i,j)\in\mathrm{bonds}} k_{ij}
  \left(\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{ij}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}\right)^2 ,
\end{equation}
where $k_{ij}$ is in $\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}\,nm^{-2}}$,
$\kappa_E=2.914952774272~\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}/E_{\mathrm{up}}$, and
$\kappa_L=10~\text{\AA}/\mathrm{nm}$.  The runtime carrier bond uses
$k_{\mathrm{CGLD}}=2k_{\mathrm{proj}}$ to keep the numerical direction vector
well constrained under tabulated torques.  For the current DOPC reference
geometry this gives $\ell=11.139~\text{\AA}$,
$M_{\mathrm{CGLD}}=77.049~\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$,
$k_{\mathrm{proj}}=39.436~E_{\mathrm{up}}\text{\AA}^{-2}$, and
$k_{\mathrm{CGLD}}=78.872~E_{\mathrm{up}}\text{\AA}^{-2}$.

\subsection{Underlying Dry-MARTINI Energy}

All lipid-environment tables are built from explicit dry-MARTINI bead
geometries.  For bead pair $i,j$ separated by $d$,
\begin{equation}
  u_{ij}(d) =
  4\epsilon_{ij}\left[\left(\frac{\sigma_{ij}}{d}\right)^{12}
  -\left(\frac{\sigma_{ij}}{d}\right)^6\right]
  + \frac{k_e q_i q_j}{d},
  \qquad d < r_c ,
\end{equation}
and $u_{ij}=0$ outside the dry-MARTINI nonbonded cutoff
$r_c=1.2~\mathrm{nm}$.  Bead types, Lennard-Jones parameters, charges, and
masses are read from the dry-MARTINI topology.  Training artifacts remain in
native dry-MARTINI units; conversion to Upside units is applied only when
tables are written to or read from simulation HDF5 files.

The table builder evaluates this direct bead energy on rigidly rotated
geometries.  It does not relax hidden beads, soften Lennard-Jones cores, add
WCA terms, subtract isotropic backgrounds, remove attractive controls, scale
specific interactions, or cap forces.  Exact bead coincidence is avoided only
by the numerical singularity guard
\begin{equation}
  d_{\mathrm{eval}}=\max(d,10^{-6}~\mathrm{nm}),
\end{equation}
which is recorded in each HDF5 group as a numerical zero guard.  This guard is
far below any physical bead radius and is not a model parameter.

\subsection{Unresolved Coordinates and Spline Transform}

The runtime CGL state resolves only $\mathbf{R}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$.
Rotations of the 14-bead DOPC reference around $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$, and
azimuthal directions that are not determined by a runtime angle, are integrated
out during table generation.  For a fixed runtime coordinate $x$, let
$U(x,\omega)$ be the explicit dry-MARTINI energy for unresolved coordinate
$\omega$ with quadrature weight $w_\omega$.  The builder uses either the
weighted energy expectation
\begin{equation}
  \bar{U}(x)=
  \frac{\sum_\omega w_\omega U(x,\omega)}
       {\sum_\omega w_\omega},
\end{equation}
or the finite-temperature potential of mean force
\begin{equation}
  F_\tau(x)=
  -k_B\tau\log
  \frac{\sum_\omega w_\omega\exp[-U(x,\omega)/(k_B\tau)]}
       {\sum_\omega w_\omega}.
\end{equation}
Here $\tau$ is an averaging temperature in Upside temperature units.  The
finite-temperature form is used when the unresolved coordinate represents a
transferable two-body free energy; the expectation form is used when the
direct averaged energy is the intended table.

CGL spline tables store a numerically conditioned control surface rather than
raw energies:
\begin{equation}
  y(x)=\log\left[1+\frac{E(x)-E_0}{k_BT_0}\right],
  \qquad
  E_0=\min_x E(x),
\end{equation}
where $E(x)$ is either $\bar{U}(x)$ or $F_\tau(x)$ and
$T_0=0.8647$ is the production transform temperature.  Runtime evaluation
applies the exact inverse
\begin{equation}
  E(x)=E_0+k_BT_0\left[\exp(y(x))-1\right],
\end{equation}
with derivatives multiplied by $k_BT_0\exp(y)$.  This transformation preserves
the physical two-body surface while making both attractive wells and steep
repulsive cores representable by single-precision spline coefficients.

\subsection{CGL--CGL Potential}

For two CGL particles define
$\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{12}=(\mathbf{R}_2-\mathbf{R}_1)/r$ and
\begin{equation}
  a_1=-\hat{\mathbf{n}}_1\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{12},\qquad
  a_2= \hat{\mathbf{n}}_2\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{12}.
\end{equation}
The CGL--CGL runtime potential is a full tensor-product spline,
\begin{equation}
  U_{\mathrm{CGL-CGL}}(r,a_1,a_2)
  =
  E_0+k_BT_0\left[
  \exp\left(
  \sum_{ijk} c_{ijk}B_i(r)B_j(a_1)B_k(a_2)
  \right)-1\right].
\end{equation}
The runtime potential has exactly three scalar coordinates:
$r,a_1,a_2$.  It has no twisting coordinate and no standalone orientation
term.  Axial bead-frame rotations and azimuthal samples are unresolved
coordinates in the table generation integral.

For each sampled $(r,a_1,a_2)$, two canonical 14-bead DOPC molecules are
rigidly rotated to the corresponding CGL directions and translated to the
specified separation.  The table averages over four azimuthal directions for
each CGL direction and eight axial bead-frame rotations for each lipid.  The
unresolved-coordinate average is a tempered two-body PMF with
$\tau=10.0$ in Upside temperature units.  This retains the direct dry-MARTINI
lipid-lipid energy while representing the free energy of the unresolved axial
degrees of freedom in the single-vector representation.  The spline coordinates
are local pair coordinates, so the table is rotationally invariant and does not
assume a global membrane normal.

The installed CGL--CGL group uses schema
\texttt{cg\_lipid\_pair\_full\_v1}, $120$ radial controls, $9$ controls for
each angular coordinate, radial spacing $0.35~\text{\AA}$, and cutoff
$41.3~\text{\AA}$.  The radial support is derived from twice the maximum
resolved DOPC bead radius plus the dry-MARTINI cutoff, so long tail-tail
contacts are included even when the CGL centers are far apart.  The stored
control quantity is the log1p-reduced tempered free energy.  Metadata records
zero fit-relaxation steps, no excluded-area term, and zero excluded-area rows.

\subsection{SC--CGL Potential}

Protein side chains interact with CGL particles through the same direct
resolved-geometry construction.  Each Upside side-chain state supplies a
center and direction for a dry-MARTINI side-chain bead geometry.  For an
SC--CGL pair define
\begin{equation}
  a_{\mathrm{SC}}=-\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\mathrm{SC}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}},
  \qquad
  a_{\mathrm{CGL}}=\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\mathrm{CGL}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}},
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$ points from the side-chain center to the CGL center.
The table is a full tensor over
$(r,a_{\mathrm{SC}},a_{\mathrm{CGL}})$ with the same inverse log1p transform
used for CGL--CGL.

For each residue type, the builder places all dry-MARTINI side-chain beads
from the rotamer library and all DOPC beads from the canonical CGL geometry.
It samples four azimuthal directions, one side-chain bead-frame rotation, and
eight CGL bead-frame rotations.  Rotamer probabilities enter as quadrature
weights.  The accepted SC--CGL table uses the direct weighted energy
expectation over unresolved coordinates rather than a separable radial/angular
factorization.

The installed SC--CGL group uses schema
\texttt{cg\_lipid\_sc\_full\_tensor\_v1}, $21$ radial controls, $11$ controls
for each angular coordinate, radial spacing $1.4~\text{\AA}$, and cutoff
$26.6~\text{\AA}$.  The stored quantity is the log1p-reduced energy
expectation.  Runtime nodes evaluate this table for CGL contacts with
side-chain placements and pass the resulting energy rows into the Upside
rotamer machinery; the interaction is not normalized by the number of
neighboring lipids.

\subsection{CGL--Particle Potential}

Non-side-chain point particles, including backbone proxy sites, ions, and
other dry-MARTINI target types, interact with CGL through a direct
DOPC-target PMF.  For a target at separation vector $\mathbf{r}$,
\begin{equation}
  a_{\mathrm{CGL}}=
  \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\mathrm{CGL}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}} .
\end{equation}
The runtime potential is a two-dimensional spline over $(r,a_{\mathrm{CGL}})$
with the inverse log1p transform.  For each target type, the builder evaluates
the explicit energy between the 14 DOPC beads and one target particle of that
type, averages over four CGL azimuthal directions and one CGL bead-frame
rotation, and stores a production-temperature PMF.

The installed CGL--particle group uses schema \texttt{cg\_lipid\_target\_v1},
$78$ radial controls, $321$ angular controls, radial spacing
$0.35~\text{\AA}$, and cutoff $26.6~\text{\AA}$.  Charged targets use this
same direct physical table as all other target types.  There is no separate
charged-backbone or ion excluded-volume projection, and no nonnegative row
projection is applied.

\subsection{SC--Particle Potential for Full-Resolution Lipid Simulations}

When lipids are kept as explicit dry-MARTINI particles rather than collapsed
to CGL, protein side chains interact with environment particles through a
direct side-chain-particle table.  For each residue type, target type, rotamer,
radius $r$, and angle
\begin{equation}
  a_{\mathrm{SC}}=
  \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\mathrm{SC}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}},
\end{equation}
the builder evaluates the dry-MARTINI nonbonded energy between the resolved
side-chain beads and the target particle.  The active runtime representation
is the full radial-angular grid
\texttt{rotamer\_full\_energy\_kj\_mol}; separable radial and angular datasets
are retained only as legacy metadata and are not the physical validation path.

The installed SC--particle group uses schema
\texttt{martini\_sc\_combined\_v1}, $96$ radial samples from $0.25$ to
$1.20~\mathrm{nm}$ and $13$ angular samples.  The stored quantity is direct
rotamer free energy in dry-MARTINI units, with a full radial-angular runtime
representation.  The table is converted to Upside units during runtime
evaluation.  No SC-environment force cap is used.

\subsection{Runtime Interaction Ownership}

The hybrid Hamiltonian assigns each interaction class to one owner so that
physical terms are neither omitted nor double counted:
\begin{itemize}
  \item CGL--CGL interactions are evaluated only by the
  \texttt{cg\_lipid\_pair} table.
  \item CGL--side-chain interactions are evaluated by the
  \texttt{cg\_lipid\_sc} table and coupled to the Upside rotamer solver.
  \item CGL--non-side-chain particle interactions are evaluated by the
  CGL--particle target table.
  \item CGLD has no nonbonded interactions; it only carries the CGL direction.
  \item Full-resolution dry-MARTINI lipid particles use the generic
  dry-MARTINI particle potential and the direct SC--particle table.
  \item Protein backbone-environment and side-chain-environment interface
  interactions remain active in both CGL and full-resolution lipid workflows.
\end{itemize}
Backbone proxy sites provide dry-MARTINI target positions for environment
coupling, while forces are projected back to the Upside backbone carriers.
Electrostatics and generic dry-MARTINI particle interactions follow the
existing Upside runtime ownership for those terms.  All CGL-specific
interactions are read from HDF5 spline tables; no analytic CGL Lennard-Jones
or orientation correction is introduced at runtime.

\subsection{Numerical Implementation}

Spline coefficients are stored in HDF5 before simulation and loaded by the
Upside C++ runtime.  CGL--CGL and SC--CGL use cubic tensor-product splines over
radius and two angular coordinates; CGL--particle uses a cubic tensor-product
spline over radius and one angular coordinate.  The angular coordinate is
clamped to $[-1,1]$.  At radii below the first sampled shell, the runtime uses
the first in-domain radial slope rather than flattening the force.  This
preserves the repulsive dry-MARTINI core represented in the table and avoids a
numerical force cap.

The HDF5 metadata is part of the model contract.  Current accepted tables
record zero fit-relaxation steps, a $10^{-6}~\mathrm{nm}$ numerical distance
guard, log1p-reduced transforms for CGL spline tables, and zero excluded-area
rows.  Preparation code rejects stale CGL tables that carry old capping,
excluded-area, relaxation, or missing-transform metadata.

Independent table slices are parallelized during generation, but HDF5 writes
remain in the parent process.  Worker count is controlled by an environment
variable or by the active Slurm CPU allocation.
Parallelization changes only wall-clock time; it does not alter the quadrature,
energy expression, or fitted surface.

\subsection{Validation Criteria}

The physical table set is validated on both isolated CGL bilayers and hybrid
protein-bilayer systems because those are the target applications tested here.
For bilayer simulations, acceptance requires a stable bilayer, CGL directions
aligned with the local leaflet normals, no persistent same-leaflet collapse, no
lipid leaflet crossings, stable protein radius of gyration, stable hydrogen-bond
counts, and no loss of protein secondary structure during production.  This is
a validation criterion for bilayer geometry, not a constraint built into the CGL
potential: the runtime interactions depend on local pair distances and local
direction-vector projections.  The current no-floor validation matrix passes
for 1RKL and 1AFO in both CGL and full-resolution lipid workflows.  In CGL
mode, final same-leaflet nearest neighbor fifth percentiles are at least
$6.75~\text{\AA}$ and orientation fifth percentiles remain above $0.86$ after
equilibration.  In full-resolution lipid mode, DOPC leaflet separation and
head-tail $|dz|$ distributions remain stable while protein hydrogen-bond and
radius-of-gyration observables fluctuate around production basins.

\subsection{Units}

Upside uses Angstroms for length and $E_{\mathrm{up}}$ for energy, with
\begin{equation}
  1~E_{\mathrm{up}}=2.914952774272~\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}},
  \qquad 1~\mathrm{nm}=10~\text{\AA}.
\end{equation}
Dry-MARTINI source parameters and side-chain training artifacts remain in
native dry-MARTINI units.  Conversion factors are explicit in table generation
and runtime loading so that HDF5 training artifacts are not silently rewritten
into Upside units.
