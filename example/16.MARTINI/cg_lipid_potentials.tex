\title{A Table-Projected One-Particle dryMARTINI Membrane for Hybrid Upside Protein Simulations}
\author{}
\date{}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We present a one-particle representation of dryMARTINI DOPC for hybrid
protein--membrane simulations in Upside.  Each coarse-grained lipid (CGL)
carries a center, a head-to-tail orientation, and a scalar tail-compression
state.  Conservative CGL-CGL, side-chain--CGL (SC-CGL), backbone--CGL, and
environment interactions are projected from dryMARTINI constituent
interactions before simulation and stored as HDF5 spline tables.  A tempered
Boltzmann projection at 25 Upside temperature units prevents a small number of
low-energy conformers from dominating the reduced tables.  Pair-conditioned
tail relaxation supplies the steric relief required when two DOPC molecules
approach, while SC-CGL endpoint corrections are derived from the corresponding
single-lipid tail-relaxation problem.  No bilayer-thickness restraint,
orientational aligning field, force cap, or interaction exclusion is used.
The conservative model is separated from the dynamical mapping.  All Upside
and CGL forces are evaluated with one global step of
$0.004\,T_{\mathrm{up}}=40$ ps, protein carrier masses remain
$1\,m_{\mathrm{up}}$, and only CGL translation is calibrated with a mass
scale and a fluctuation--dissipation-consistent two-mode generalized Langevin
equation.  A 404-lipid membrane gives a coarse-graining-corrected long-lag
lateral diffusion coefficient of $11.45$--$11.47~\mu\mathrm{m}^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$,
consistent with DOPC measurements near 303 K.  Independent 1RKL and 1AFO
trajectories remain finite through four production segments, preserve protein
secondary structure, retain their initial protein--bilayer orientations, and
maintain closed, ordered bilayers.  The resulting implementation preserves the
committed protein force field and changes only the CGL transport mapping.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The MARTINI family reduces molecular detail sufficiently to access membrane
length and time scales beyond routine atomistic simulation.
\cite{marrink2007}  Dry Martini removes explicit solvent while retaining a
coarse-grained lipid Hamiltonian suitable for membrane simulations.
\cite{arnarez2015}  Coupling that environment to Upside presents a distinct
multiscale problem.  The protein must continue to use the trained Upside force
field, whereas every protein--environment and environment--environment
interaction must remain active and traceable to dryMARTINI source physics.

The present model further maps all 14 dryMARTINI particles in one DOPC
molecule to one oriented CGL object.  A useful one-particle membrane must be
both thermodynamically coherent and dynamically interpretable.  An isotropic
center cannot encode the head--tail asymmetry of a phospholipid, and a rigid
oriented center cannot reproduce the relief obtained when resolved lipid tails
compress in a contact.  Conversely, fitting a membrane thickness or adding an
aligning field would make the force field depend on the desired bilayer rather
than on the represented molecule.

We therefore separate three questions.  First, conservative interactions are
projected from resolved dryMARTINI particles to CGL coordinates and tabulated.
Second, unresolved tail relaxation is represented by source-derived energy
differences and a scalar internal coordinate.  Third, translational transport
is calibrated independently with a CGL-only generalized Langevin equation
(GLE).  This division prevents a transport correction from changing protein
dynamics or compensating for an incorrect conservative potential.

The implementation follows the native Upside runtime contract: all
simulation-time conservative interactions are spline lookups from HDF5.  The
paper describes the force-field derivation, hybrid mapping, dynamical time
mapping, and validation tests in the same order in which they enter the model.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Units and Table-Only Runtime Contract}

Force-field sources and intermediate training arrays remain in native
dryMARTINI units: nm, kJ mol$^{-1}$, and elementary charge.  Conversion to
Upside uses
\begin{equation}
1~E_{\mathrm{up}}=2.914952774272~\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}},
\qquad
1~\mathrm{nm}=10~\angstrom,
\end{equation}
and is recorded in HDF5 attributes.  No conversion factor is baked into a
native dryMARTINI training artifact.

For constituent particles $a$ and $b$, the source nonbonded energy is
\begin{equation}
u_{ab}(r)=4\epsilon_{ab}
\left[\left(\frac{\sigma_{ab}}{r}\right)^{12}
-\left(\frac{\sigma_{ab}}{r}\right)^6\right]
+\frac{k_e q_aq_b}{r},
\qquad r<1.2~\mathrm{nm},
\label{eq:source_pair}
\end{equation}
with the source cutoff convention applied during table construction.  Even
this simple radial interaction is evaluated at runtime from
\texttt{particle.h5}, not from a hard-coded Lennard-Jones expression.  The
installed particle artifact contains 81 unique $(\epsilon,\sigma,q_aq_b)$
rows on 1000-point radial grids.

The three force-field artifacts have distinct roles:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{particle.h5} stores generic dryMARTINI particle-pair tables;
\item \texttt{sidechain.h5} stores rotamer-resolved Upside side-chain versus
      dryMARTINI particle tables; and
\item \texttt{dopc.h5} stores CGL-CGL, SC-CGL, CGL-particle, internal
      compaction, and orientation metadata.
\end{itemize}
At preparation time these artifacts populate the runtime nodes
\path{martini_potential}, \path{martini_sc_table_1body},
\path{cg_lipid_pair}, \path{cg_lipid_rotamer_sc}, and
\path{cg_lipid_target}.  The interface does not disable SC-environment,
backbone-environment, SC-CGL, or CGL-CGL interactions.

\subsection{One-Particle DOPC Coordinates}

The 14 dryMARTINI particles of DOPC are mapped to a center
$\mathbf R_i$ and a unit orientation $\hat{\mathbf n}_i$.  The orientation
follows the DOPC head-to-tail axis defined by the NC3 head group and the
midpoint of the terminal tail particles.  At runtime,
\texttt{cgl\_orientation\_state} propagates $\hat{\mathbf n}_i$, and
\texttt{compose\_vector6d} exposes
$(\mathbf R_i,\hat{\mathbf n}_i)$ to the spline potentials.

The installed source ensemble gives a center-to-tail projection of
$9.406~\angstrom$ and a transverse rotational inertia of
$2.2597\times10^4~\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}\,\angstrom^2}$.  Both are calculated from
the constituent DOPC geometry and masses.  They are not fitted to a target
bilayer orientation.

\subsection{Tempered Projection of Hidden Coordinates}

Each CGL table averages over unresolved DOPC conformers, bead-frame choices,
azimuths, and rotamer states as appropriate to the interacting objects.  For
resolved hidden coordinate $\omega$ and retained coordinate $x$, the projected
free energy is
\begin{equation}
F_{\tau}(x)=-k_B\tau
\log\left[
\frac{\sum_{\omega}w_{\omega}
\exp[-U(x,\omega)/(k_B\tau)]}
{\sum_{\omega}w_{\omega}}
\right].
\label{eq:tempered_projection}
\end{equation}
The production tables retain $\tau=25$ in Upside temperature units.  This
tempered Boltzmann average is deliberately broader than the production
temperature.  It prevents rare, exceptionally favorable hidden conformers
from producing localized attraction and CGL clustering while preserving the
directional dependence of the constituent interactions.

The spline control values use the invertible transform
\begin{equation}
y(x)=\log\left[1+\frac{F_{\tau}(x)-F_{\min}}{k_BT_0}\right],
\qquad
F_{\tau}(x)=F_{\min}+k_BT_0[\exp y(x)-1],
\label{eq:table_transform}
\end{equation}
where $T_0=0.8647~T_{\mathrm{up}}$.  The transform improves interpolation of
the repulsive range without changing the represented energy.

\subsection{CGL-CGL Directional Table and Tail Relaxation}

The base CGL pair coordinate is
\begin{equation}
x_{ij}=(r_{ij},a_i,a_j),\qquad
a_i=-\hat{\mathbf n}_i\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij},\qquad
a_j= \hat{\mathbf n}_j\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij}.
\end{equation}
The installed \texttt{cg\_lipid\_pair} table has 140 radial controls and
9 controls on each angular axis.  Its uncorrected surface is the tempered
projection of all constituent DOPC-DOPC interactions in Eq.~\ref{eq:source_pair}.

A rigid constituent projection overestimates the cost of close tail contacts
because resolved DOPC tails can shorten and rearrange.  The pair correction is
therefore derived from two energies at every sampled pair coordinate.  The
rigid free energy $F_{\mathrm{rigid}}(x_{ij})$ retains the reference tails.
The relaxed free energy $F_{\mathrm{rel}}(x_{ij})$ minimizes sampled axial tail
compression while including both the dryMARTINI contact energy and the
intramolecular cost of the deformed DOPC conformers.  The correction is
\begin{equation}
\Delta F_{\mathrm{pair}}(x_{ij})=
M(x_{ij})\min\left[0,
F_{\mathrm{rel}}(x_{ij})-F_{\mathrm{rigid}}(x_{ij})\right],
\label{eq:pair_relax}
\end{equation}
where $M$ restricts the relief to face-to-face tail contacts.  The installed
artifact uses a 2.5 nm radial support and a face-cosine threshold of 0.5.
Equation~\ref{eq:pair_relax} can only remove repulsion that is relieved by a
physical tail deformation; it cannot create a fitted bilayer attraction.

The correction is incorporated into the stored CGL-CGL spline.  The runtime
pair-compaction overlay is disabled, so CGL-CGL energy is evaluated once from
the pair-relaxed table rather than being counted again through the scalar
compaction state.

\subsection{Scalar Tail-Compression State and SC-CGL Coupling}

Protein contact can select a different DOPC tail geometry even when the CGL
center and orientation are unchanged.  A scalar state $q_i$ represents this
remaining internal response.  Its physical source coordinate is the negative
DOPC head-to-tail extension, so increasing $q_i$ corresponds to a shorter
tail.  An affine mapping places the extended and compact representatives at
$q=0$ and $q=1$; the installed compressed endpoint is $q=1.209$.

The internal free energy $F_{\mathrm{self}}(q)$ is obtained from a 256-conformer
isolated-DOPC intramolecular Monte Carlo ensemble and stored as a 14-control
spline in \texttt{cg\_lipid\_compaction}.  The runtime state has mass
$17.670~m_{\mathrm{up}}$ and thermostat time $1.0~T_{\mathrm{up}}$.  It is a
physical molecular internal coordinate with a source-derived self PMF; it is
not computed from leaflet identity, bilayer thickness, or distance to a
membrane plane.

For side-chain placement bead $A$, the endpoint relief follows the same
source-conditioned construction as Eq.~\ref{eq:pair_relax}:
\begin{equation}
\Delta F_A^{(s)}(r,a)=
\min\left[0,F_{A,\mathrm{rel}}^{(s)}(r,a)
-F_{A,\mathrm{rigid}}^{(s)}(r,a)\right],
\qquad s\in\{\mathrm{ext},\mathrm{comp},\mathrm{compr}\}.
\label{eq:sc_relax}
\end{equation}
The relaxed energy is the reduction in positive tail-bead nonbonded overlap
available to a tail deformation at that endpoint.  The production SC-CGL
energy interpolates the extended, compact, and compressed endpoint splines
with $q_i$.  The installed table contains 29 side-chain placement-bead rows,
25 radial controls, and 11 controls on each directional angular axis.

The generic CGL-particle table contains 38 dryMARTINI target types on 91 radial
and 321 angular samples.  It remains a direct constituent projection without a
$q$-dependent overlay because no independent target-conditioned internal
relaxation term is required by the accepted model.  Backbone--CGL and other
target interactions therefore remain active through their base physical
tables; they are not turned off or replaced by restraints.

\subsection{Hybrid Protein Mapping}

The protein is not converted to a dryMARTINI protein.  Upside retains its
backbone and rotamer force field.  For each complete residue, the hybrid
mapping records the Upside backbone carriers and a virtual dryMARTINI-like
backbone proxy formed from those carriers.  Side-chain--environment energies
enter the existing rotamer solver through
\texttt{martini\_sc\_table\_1body} and
\texttt{cg\_lipid\_rotamer\_sc}; backbone and generic environment energies
enter through \texttt{martini\_potential} and
\texttt{cg\_lipid\_target}.  The virtual proxy transmits forces to the Upside
carriers through the stored mapping.

Protein carrier masses are fixed at exactly $1~m_{\mathrm{up}}$.  Neither the
protein masses, the trained protein force field, nor the protein thermostat is
rescaled to match membrane transport.  Internal side-chain optimization may
occur within an Upside stage, but SC-CGL forces are part of the same global
force evaluation as CGL-CGL and backbone forces.

\subsection{Global Time Step and CGL-Only GLE}

One standard velocity-Verlet stage advances the full system by
\begin{equation}
\Delta t=0.004~T_{\mathrm{up}}=40~\mathrm{ps}.
\label{eq:timestep}
\end{equation}
There is no multiplier between requested steps and integrated steps.  Thus
$1~T_{\mathrm{up}}=10$ ns, and CGL-CGL, SC-CGL, backbone--CGL, and protein
forces all act on the same physical clock.

The native DOPC mass is $1008~\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}=84~m_{\mathrm{up}}$.  The
accepted translational mass scale is 0.05, giving
$m_{\mathrm{CGL}}=4.2~m_{\mathrm{up}}$.  A two-mode Markovian embedding of an
exponential-memory GLE replaces the ordinary translational thermostat only for
CGL particles.  At $T_0$, the memory times and couplings are
\begin{equation}
(\tau_1,\tau_2)=(0.2,2.0)~T_{\mathrm{up}},\qquad
(g_1,g_2)=(0.30375,0.2205).
\label{eq:gle}
\end{equation}
The auxiliary momenta are propagated with the fluctuation--dissipation noise
and are stored in every checkpoint.  The CGL orientation uses the
source-derived transverse inertia and a rotational thermostat time of
$0.008~T_{\mathrm{up}}$.  None of these settings acts on protein carriers.
Colored-noise embeddings of this form provide a controlled separation between
the conservative potential and dynamical friction.\cite{ceriotti2010}

\subsection{Diffusion Mapping and Analysis}

Lateral positions are unwrapped across the periodic cell.  At every frame the
instantaneous center-of-mass displacement of the CGL population is removed.
For lag $t$, the raw two-dimensional diffusion estimate follows
\begin{equation}
D_{\mathrm{CGL}}=\frac{1}{4}\frac{d}{dt}
\left\langle|\mathbf R_{xy}(t_0+t)-\mathbf R_{xy}(t_0)|^2\right\rangle_{t_0,i}.
\label{eq:diffusion}
\end{equation}
The model uses the prescribed temporal coarse-graining correction
\begin{equation}
D_{\mathrm{DOPC}}=(14\times4)D_{\mathrm{CGL}}=56D_{\mathrm{CGL}},
\label{eq:cg_factor}
\end{equation}
where 14 is the number of dryMARTINI particles represented by one CGL and 4 is
the MARTINI particle coarse-graining factor.  Because
$1~\angstrom^2/T_{\mathrm{up}}=1~\mu\mathrm{m}^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ under
Eq.~\ref{eq:timestep}, the numerical conversion is direct after multiplication
by 56.  The factor modifies the interpreted diffusion coefficient, not the
Upside simulation clock.

Bilayer validation also measures the mean leaflet-center separation,
$|n_z|$, lateral occupancy, low-wavevector density modes, nearest-neighbor
spacing, leaflet reassignment, and finite coordinates and energies.  Hybrid
validation additionally measures hydrogen-bond score, C$\alpha$ RMSD after
rigid alignment, DSSP agreement, helix fraction, and the angle between each
protein transmembrane axis and the bilayer plane.  No protein-angle restraint
is present.

\subsection{Simulation Protocol}

The transport calibration used a fresh 404-CGL DOPC bilayer at
$T=0.8647~T_{\mathrm{up}}\simeq303$ K.  The run contained 30,000 global steps,
or $120~T_{\mathrm{up}}=1.2~\mu$s.  Structural and multi-origin diffusion
statistics discarded the initial 20\%, leaving $96~T_{\mathrm{up}}$.

Hybrid validation used fresh 1RKL and 1AFO preparations with 275 and 174 CGL
particles, respectively.  Each system was propagated through stage 7.0 and
three sequential production continuations.  Every segment contained 2000
steps ($8~T_{\mathrm{up}}=80$ ns), for a total of 320 ns per protein.  The
committed workflow minimizes each continuation input before dynamics;
trajectory statistics are evaluated within segments so that minimization
displacements are not interpreted as diffusion.  CGL orientation, compaction,
GLE auxiliary momenta, and ordinary momenta are restored from the preceding
checkpoint.  Production burn-in restraints and protein orientation restraints
are zero.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Bilayer Structure and Lateral Transport}

The 404-CGL membrane remained closed and laterally uniform.  After the initial
relaxation, the mean leaflet-center separation was $16.52~\angstrom$ and the
final separation was $16.41~\angstrom$.  The mean orientational order was
$\langle|n_z|\rangle=0.9924$, with fifth percentile 0.9770.  Six-by-six
occupancy grids had no empty cells in either leaflet; low-wavevector density
amplitudes averaged 0.0158 and 0.0156 for the two leaflets.  The maximum
leaflet reassignment fraction was 0.00495 and did not grow into mixing or
ejection.

Table~\ref{tab:diffusion} reports the diffusion estimates.  The short-lag
window is subdiffusive ($\alpha=0.657$ in
$\mathrm{MSD}\propto t^{\alpha}$) and gives the largest value.  At longer
lags, the estimates converge: the 10--40 and 20--60 $T_{\mathrm{up}}$ windows
give $11.47$ and $11.45~\mu\mathrm{m}^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ after the factor of
56.  Lipid14 reports DOPC values of 6.48 and
$9.49~\mu\mathrm{m}^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ in NPT and NVE simulations at 303 K
and cites an experimental value of $11.5~\mu\mathrm{m}^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ at
303 K.\cite{dickson2014}  An independent DOPC study reports approximately
$9.32~\mu\mathrm{m}^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ at 298 K.\cite{ermilova2020}  The
long-lag CGL result therefore closes the intended DOPC time scale without a
change to the Upside clock.

\subsection{Protein Structure and Orientation}

Protein observables remain within the committed stable envelope
(Table~\ref{tab:protein}).  For 1RKL, the mean and final hydrogen-bond scores
are 37.62 and 35.67 compared with 30.38 initially.  Mean and final DSSP matches
are 0.925 and 0.903, and the final helix fraction is 0.710 compared with 0.742
initially.  The protein--bilayer angle averages $35.90^\circ$ around an initial
$34.42^\circ$ and spans $28.83^\circ$--$39.51^\circ$; it does not rotate to a
vertical state.

The two 1AFO helices retain a mean DSSP match of 0.989 and a final match of
1.000.  Their orientation ranges remain close to the starting geometry:
$12.76^\circ$--$18.94^\circ$ for chain A and
$14.39^\circ$--$18.24^\circ$ for chain B.  These fluctuations occur without an
orientational restraint and therefore result from the unchanged protein force
field and the physical hybrid interaction tables.

\subsection{Clock and Restart Verification}

Every 2000-step hybrid segment records exactly $8.0~T_{\mathrm{up}}$, and the
30,000-step membrane trajectory records exactly $120~T_{\mathrm{up}}$.  The
prepared hybrid files contain protein carrier mass 1, CGL mass 4.2, the two
GLE modes in Eq.~\ref{eq:gle}, and all required conservative interaction
nodes.  CGL orientation and compaction restart errors are at single-precision
roundoff or zero.  These checks rule out the earlier three-integrations-per-step
scheduler and confirm that the transport calibration does not slow the Upside
protein core.

\section{Discussion}

The principal modeling result is the separation of conservative reduction from
dynamical coarse graining.  The tempered constituent projection and physical
tail-relaxation energies determine membrane structure and protein contact
forces.  The CGL mass and GLE determine how rapidly that conservative surface
is explored.  Changing the protein mass, changing the global time unit, or
weakening SC-CGL interactions would mix those roles and would not constitute a
physical transport calibration.

The tail treatment also avoids membrane-specific fitting.  Pair relaxation is
calculated from the energetic benefit and molecular cost of deforming two DOPC
tails at a specified pair geometry.  The SC-CGL correction is calculated from
the corresponding side-chain-conditioned tail relief.  The scalar state has an
isolated-DOPC self PMF.  None of these terms contains a target leaflet
separation or protein angle.  Bilayer closure and protein orientation are
therefore validation observables rather than force-field inputs.

The long-lag diffusion plateau is the appropriate clock diagnostic.  Short-lag
motion is subdiffusive because a CGL remains caged by neighboring lipids; in
protein-containing systems, crowding and direct contacts add further
subdiffusion.  That local slowing does not imply a different timestep for
SC-CGL interactions.  All conservative forces are evaluated at the same
40-ps global stage, while the protein-free membrane supplies the transferable
bulk transport calibration.

The present validation is limited to DOPC, 303 K, and two membrane proteins.
Other lipid chemistries require new constituent projections and source-derived
internal relaxation surfaces.  Quantitative hydrodynamic finite-size analysis
and a larger protein panel would be appropriate before using the model for
absolute kinetic predictions outside this regime.

\section{Conclusions}

The accepted dryMARTINI--Upside interface is a single physical model rather
than a sequence of compensating patches.  It uses source-projected spline
tables for every conservative interaction, retains the clustering-resistant
tempered Boltzmann average, represents unresolved DOPC tail relaxation in both
CGL-CGL and SC-CGL contacts, and keeps generic target interactions active.
Transport is corrected only on CGL particles with a mass scale and an
FDT-consistent GLE.  The Upside backbone remains on its original mass, force
field, thermostat, and 40-ps global integration step.

The 404-lipid membrane reproduces the experimental-scale DOPC diffusion rate
while remaining closed, ordered, and laterally uniform.  Fresh 1RKL and 1AFO
trajectories remain finite through stage 7.3, preserve secondary structure,
and fluctuate around their initial bilayer orientations without restraints.
Together these tests establish that the corrected CGL time mapping fixes the
frozen-bilayer symptom without degrading the committed protein dynamics.
