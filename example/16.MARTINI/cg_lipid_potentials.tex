\section{Single-Vector dry-MARTINI Lipid Model}

\subsection{Coarse Lipid State}

Each dry-MARTINI DOPC molecule is represented by one oriented coarse lipid
particle, CGL.  Its position $\mathbf{R}$ is the geometric center of the
14 dry-MARTINI DOPC beads.  Its direction $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ is the unit vector
from the NC3 head bead to the midpoint of the terminal C5A and C5B tail beads.
The canonical resolved DOPC geometry used for table generation is centered at
$\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{0}$ and rotated so that this head-to-tail vector lies on
the body-frame lipid axis.

Upside stores the direction using a companion Cartesian site, CGLD,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{CGLD}} = \mathbf{R}+\ell\hat{\mathbf{n}},
\end{equation}
with harmonic carrier energy
\begin{equation}
  U_{\mathrm{CGLD}} =
  \frac{1}{2}k_{\mathrm{CGLD}}
  \left(|\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{CGLD}}-\mathbf{R}|-\ell\right)^2 .
\end{equation}
CGLD is a numerical direction carrier, not a dry-MARTINI bead.  It has no
nonbonded interactions and no standalone orientation potential.  All CGL
orientation preferences come from the tabulated two-body interactions below.

The carrier length, mass, and bond stiffness are derived from the canonical
DOPC geometry and bonded topology.  With centered bead positions
$\mathbf{x}_i$, bead masses $m_i$, and tail midpoint
$\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{tail}}$,
\begin{align}
  \ell &= \left|\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{tail}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}\right|,\\
  I_\perp &= \sum_i m_i
  \left(|\mathbf{x}_i|^2-(\mathbf{x}_i\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}})^2\right),\\
  M_{\mathrm{CGLD}} &= I_\perp/\ell^2 .
\end{align}
The projected dry-MARTINI bonded stiffness is
\begin{equation}
  k_{\mathrm{proj}} =
  \frac{1}{\kappa_E\kappa_L^2}
  \sum_{(i,j)\in\mathrm{bonds}} k_{ij}
  \left(\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{ij}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}\right)^2 ,
\end{equation}
where $k_{ij}$ is in $\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}\,nm^{-2}}$,
$\kappa_E=2.914952774272~\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}/E_{\mathrm{up}}$, and
$\kappa_L=10~\text{\AA}/\mathrm{nm}$.  Runtime uses
$k_{\mathrm{CGLD}}=2k_{\mathrm{proj}}$ for a stiff numerical direction
carrier.  For the DOPC reference geometry,
\[
  \ell=11.139~\text{\AA},\quad
  M_{\mathrm{CGLD}}=77.049~\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}},\quad
  k_{\mathrm{CGLD}}=78.872~E_{\mathrm{up}}\text{\AA}^{-2}.
\]

\subsection{Resolved dry-MARTINI Energy}

All CGL tables are generated from explicit dry-MARTINI bead energies.  For bead
pair $i,j$ separated by distance $d$,
\begin{equation}
  u_{ij}(d) =
  4\epsilon_{ij}
  \left[\left(\frac{\sigma_{ij}}{d}\right)^{12}
  -\left(\frac{\sigma_{ij}}{d}\right)^6\right]
  +\frac{k_e q_i q_j}{d},
  \qquad d<r_c,
\end{equation}
and $u_{ij}=0$ for $d\ge r_c$, with dry-MARTINI cutoff
$r_c=1.2~\mathrm{nm}$.  Bead types, Lennard-Jones parameters, charges, masses,
and bonded topology are read from the dry-MARTINI files.

The table builder evaluates these energies on rigidly rotated canonical bead
geometries.  The CGL model integrates out unresolved rigid orientations of the
canonical bodies, not internal conformational changes of DOPC.  In particular,
the table generation does not bend DOPC tails or relax hidden beads.  The model
also does not introduce force caps, interaction scaling, nonnegative
projections, excluded-area corrections, WCA replacements, or a separate CGL
orientation term.  A numerical singularity guard,
\begin{equation}
  d_{\mathrm{eval}}=\max(d,10^{-6}~\mathrm{nm}),
\end{equation}
is used only to avoid division by zero at exact bead coincidence.

Source dry-MARTINI parameters remain in native units.  Conversion to Upside
units is explicit in table generation and runtime loading:
\begin{equation}
  1~E_{\mathrm{up}}=2.914952774272~\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}},
  \qquad 1~\mathrm{nm}=10~\text{\AA}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Hidden Orientations and Tempered PMF}

The runtime CGL state resolves only $\mathbf{R}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$.  A
rigid DOPC body with the same $\mathbf{R}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ can still be
rotated around $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$.  Pair coordinates also leave unresolved
azimuthal placements of direction vectors around the pair axis.  These
unresolved rigid-body coordinates are denoted collectively by $\omega$.

For a runtime coordinate $x$, let $U(x,\omega)$ be the explicit bead energy for
one hidden orientation and let $w_\omega$ be its quadrature weight.  The model
uses a tempered potential of mean force for all hidden-orientation reductions:
\begin{equation}
  F_\tau(x)=
  -k_B\tau
  \log
  \frac{\sum_\omega w_\omega
  \exp[-U(x,\omega)/(k_B\tau)]}
  {\sum_\omega w_\omega},
  \qquad \tau=10.0 .
\end{equation}
The discrete sum is the quadrature approximation to integration over hidden
rigid orientations.  The weights $w_\omega$ belong to the quadrature measure;
they are not moved outside the energy.  The parameter $\tau$ controls how
strongly the log-sum-exp is dominated by low-energy hidden states.  Low
$\tau$ approaches selection of the best hidden orientation, while high $\tau$
allows more sampled orientations to contribute.  It is not a unit-conversion
factor and it does not multiply the resulting force field.

This tempering is especially important for CGL--CGL interactions in a dense
bilayer.  An ordinary production-temperature two-body PMF can be dominated by
rare favorable hidden bead-frame rotations.  In a many-neighbor membrane, each
pair cannot independently realize its own best hidden rigid orientation with a
single physical lipid body.  Tempering reduces that soft-minimum dominance and
keeps more unresolved orientational entropy in the effective pair potential.
The same tempered-PMF rule is used for CGL--CGL, SC--CGL, CGL--particle, and
SC--particle tables so that the hybrid interface has one hidden-state reduction
method.

\subsection{Spline Control Transform}

CGL tables store a conditioned control variable instead of raw energy.  If
$E(x)=F_\tau(x)$ and $E_0=\min_x E(x)$ for one type pair, the stored spline
control is
\begin{equation}
  y(x)=\log\left[1+\frac{E(x)-E_0}{k_BT_0}\right],
  \qquad T_0=0.8647 .
\end{equation}
At runtime the exact inverse is applied,
\begin{equation}
  E(x)=E_0+k_BT_0\left[\exp(y(x))-1\right],
\end{equation}
and derivatives are multiplied by $k_BT_0\exp(y)$.  This transform preserves
the physical two-body surface while representing both attractive wells and
steep repulsive cores with single-precision spline coefficients.

\subsection{CGL--CGL Table}

For two CGL particles define
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathbf{r}}_{12}=
  \frac{\mathbf{R}_2-\mathbf{R}_1}{|\mathbf{R}_2-\mathbf{R}_1|},
  \qquad
  a_1=-\hat{\mathbf{n}}_1\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{12},
  \qquad
  a_2= \hat{\mathbf{n}}_2\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{12}.
\end{equation}
The runtime potential is a full tensor-product spline over
$(r,a_1,a_2)$:
\begin{equation}
  U_{\mathrm{CGL-CGL}}(r,a_1,a_2)
  =
  E_0+k_BT_0
  \left[
  \exp\left(\sum_{ijk} c_{ijk}B_i(r)B_j(a_1)B_k(a_2)\right)-1
  \right].
\end{equation}
There is no fourth twisting coordinate.  The axial bead-frame rotations and
azimuthal placements are hidden variables integrated out during table
generation.

For each sampled $(r,a_1,a_2)$, two canonical 14-bead DOPC bodies are rigidly
rotated and translated into the corresponding pair geometry.  The quadrature
uses four azimuthal samples for each direction vector and eight axial
bead-frame rotations for each lipid.  The radial support extends to twice the
maximum resolved DOPC bead radius plus the dry-MARTINI cutoff, so tail-tail
contacts are included even when CGL centers are far apart.

The regenerated HDF5 group is \texttt{cg\_lipid\_pair} with schema
\texttt{cg\_lipid\_pair\_full}.  It stores $120$ radial controls, $9$ controls
for each angular coordinate, radial spacing $0.35~\text{\AA}$, cutoff
$41.3~\text{\AA}$, the log1p-reduced tempered free energy, and
\texttt{n\_modes=0} for the full tensor representation.

\subsection{SC--CGL Table}

Protein side chains interact with CGL particles through a direct resolved
side-chain geometry against the canonical DOPC geometry.  For an SC--CGL pair,
\begin{equation}
  a_{\mathrm{SC}}=-\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\mathrm{SC}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}},
  \qquad
  a_{\mathrm{CGL}}=\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\mathrm{CGL}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}},
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$ points from the side-chain center to the CGL center.
For residue type $s$, the table stores
\begin{equation}
  U_{s,\mathrm{CGL}}(r,a_{\mathrm{SC}},a_{\mathrm{CGL}})
  =
  E_{0,s}+k_BT_0
  \left[
  \exp\left(
  \sum_{ijk} c^{(s)}_{ijk}
  B_i(r)B_j(a_{\mathrm{SC}})B_k(a_{\mathrm{CGL}})
  \right)-1
  \right].
\end{equation}

The microscopic energy includes all dry-MARTINI side-chain beads for a rotamer
state and all 14 DOPC beads for the CGL body.  Rotamer probabilities and
bead-frame samples provide the quadrature weights in the tempered PMF.  The
table uses four azimuthal samples, one side-chain bead-frame sample, and eight
CGL bead-frame samples.  It is a full tensor over the two angular coordinates,
not a separable radial-plus-angular factorization.

The regenerated HDF5 group is \texttt{cg\_lipid\_sc} with schema
\texttt{cg\_lipid\_sc\_full\_tensor}.  It stores $18$ explicit side-chain
types, $21$ radial controls, $11$ controls for each angular coordinate,
radial spacing $1.4~\text{\AA}$, cutoff $26.6~\text{\AA}$, the
log1p-reduced tempered free energy, and \texttt{n\_modes=0}.  Runtime evaluates
this table for side-chain rotamer placements and passes the row energies to
the Upside rotamer solver.

\subsection{CGL--Particle Table}

Backbone proxy sites, ions, and other non-side-chain dry-MARTINI target types
interact with CGL through a two-dimensional direct DOPC-target table.  For a
target particle,
\begin{equation}
  a_{\mathrm{CGL}}=
  \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\mathrm{CGL}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}} .
\end{equation}
The table evaluates the dry-MARTINI energy between the 14 DOPC beads and one
target particle of each target type, then applies the same $\tau=10.0$
tempered PMF over hidden CGL rigid orientations.  Charged targets use this
same direct physical table; there is no separate ion or backbone excluded
volume correction.

The regenerated HDF5 group is \texttt{cg\_lipid\_target} with schema
\texttt{cg\_lipid\_target}.  It stores $38$ target types, $78$ radial
controls, $321$ angular controls, radial spacing $0.35~\text{\AA}$, cutoff
$26.6~\text{\AA}$, the log1p-reduced tempered free energy, and
\texttt{n\_modes=0}.

\subsection{SC--Particle Table}

Full-resolution lipid simulations retain explicit dry-MARTINI lipid beads.
In that mode, protein side chains interact with environment particles through
a direct SC--particle table.  For each residue type, target type, rotamer,
radius $r$, and angle
\begin{equation}
  a_{\mathrm{SC}}=\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\mathrm{SC}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}},
\end{equation}
the builder evaluates the dry-MARTINI energy between the resolved side-chain
beads and the target particle, then applies the same $\tau=10.0$ tempered PMF
over unresolved side-chain bead-frame samples.  The runtime representation is
a full radial-angular grid for each rotamer and target type.

The regenerated HDF5 file \texttt{sidechain.h5} uses schema
\texttt{martini\_sc\_combined}.  The \texttt{sc\_table} group stores $96$
radial samples from $0.25$ to $1.20~\mathrm{nm}$, $13$ angular samples,
\texttt{relaxation=rigid\_rotated\_geometry}, and
\texttt{azimuthal\_average=tempered\_boltzmann\_free\_energy}.  The table is
stored in dry-MARTINI units and converted to Upside units during runtime
evaluation.

\subsection{Runtime Ownership}

Each physical interaction class has one runtime owner:
\begin{itemize}
  \item CGL--CGL interactions are evaluated by \texttt{cg\_lipid\_pair}.
  \item SC--CGL interactions are evaluated by \texttt{cg\_lipid\_sc} and the
        Upside rotamer machinery.
  \item CGL--non-side-chain target interactions are evaluated by
        \texttt{cg\_lipid\_target}.
  \item CGLD has no nonbonded interactions and only carries the CGL direction.
  \item Full-resolution dry-MARTINI particles use the generic dry-MARTINI
        particle potential and the SC--particle table.
\end{itemize}
Protein side-chain and backbone interactions with the dry-MARTINI environment
remain present at full strength.  Backbone proxy sites provide dry-MARTINI
target positions for environment coupling, and their forces are projected back
to the Upside backbone carriers.  All CGL-specific interactions are read from
HDF5 spline tables; no analytic CGL Lennard-Jones term or extra orientation
correction is introduced at runtime.

\subsection{Numerical Evaluation}

Spline coefficients are stored in HDF5 before simulation and loaded by the
Upside C++ runtime.  CGL--CGL and SC--CGL use cubic tensor-product splines over
one radial and two angular coordinates.  CGL--particle uses a cubic
tensor-product spline over one radial and one angular coordinate.  Angular
coordinates are clamped to $[-1,1]$.  At radii below the first sampled shell,
runtime evaluation extrapolates from the first in-domain radial slope rather
than flattening the force, preserving the repulsive core represented by the
table.

The HDF5 metadata is part of the model contract.  Regenerated tables record
the numerical distance guard, the tempered-PMF averaging temperature, the
log1p transform metadata for CGL tables, and the full-tensor schemas described
above.  Preparation rejects CGL tables whose metadata does not match this
contract.

\subsection{Validation Scope}

The model is validated on isolated CGL bilayers and hybrid protein-bilayer
systems.  Acceptance criteria include stable bilayer packing, CGL directions
consistent with leaflet orientation, no persistent same-leaflet collapse, no
lipid leaflet crossings, stable protein radius of gyration, stable
hydrogen-bond counts, and preservation of protein secondary structure during
production.  These checks validate the bilayer applications; they are not
constraints embedded in the potential, which depends only on local pair
distances and local direction-vector projections.

The tempered PMF defines a configurational coarse-grained potential.  It does
not by itself guarantee microscopic lipid dynamics.  Dynamics-sensitive
observables such as lateral diffusion or orientation autocorrelation therefore
require comparison to reference trajectories and, if needed, an effective-time
calibration or a separate dissipative model.
