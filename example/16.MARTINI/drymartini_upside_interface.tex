\title{Composing a Machine-Learned Protein Force Field with a Physical Membrane:
An Exact Derived-Site Interface Between Upside and Full-Resolution dry-MARTINI}

\abbreviations{MD, molecular dynamics; CG, coarse-grained; HDX, hydrogen--deuterium
exchange; PMF, potential of mean force; MBAR, multistate Bennett acceptance ratio;
g-JF, Gr\o{}nbech-Jensen--Farago; RMSD, root-mean-square deviation}

\keywords{coarse-grained simulation, membrane proteins, force-field composition,
hydrogen--deuterium exchange, dry-MARTINI, Upside}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Machine-learned protein force fields are accurate where training data is abundant
and undefined where it is not; membrane proteins are the second case. We avoid
training an implicit-membrane term by \emph{composing} two independently
parameterized models---the machine-learned Upside protein force field and a
full-resolution physical dry-MARTINI bilayer---coupled through their raw
cross-interactions, with no fitting to membrane-protein data. The technical core is
the protein--membrane interface. The dry-MARTINI backbone bead is a derived site: a
mass-weighted $14{:}12{:}12{:}16$ centre of N, C$\alpha$, C, and the carbonyl~O,
where O is taken from Upside's own \texttt{infer\_H\_O} node rather than rebuilt
from a stored local frame. Because every term is then a linear combination of nodes
the engine already differentiates, the reverse pass is a constant-weight split whose
O share is handed to \texttt{infer\_H\_O} and propagated by its own chain rule. The
interface is therefore exact rather than a partial route: forces remaining on the
derived backbone and O sites are identically zero, and total momentum is conserved
to $1.7\times10^{-9}$ of the largest single force. We report the calibration
honestly. Every pair interaction is served from a spline table that reproduces the
published dry-MARTINI functional form---potential-shifted Lennard-Jones and
reaction-field electrostatics, both vanishing at 1.2~nm---to $10^{-10}$ in native
units. Whole-molecule DOPC diffusion is \emph{not} reproduced, so production uses an
explicitly labelled bare-particle mobility. An audit of the composed Hamiltonian shows
that while the sidechain environment field is correctly replaced by the explicit
lipid, three \emph{protein--protein} terms of the parent force field have no MARTINI
counterpart and are absent. We also found and corrected a systematic 0.568~\AA{}
displacement of the sidechain anchor. Neither, however, explains the model's
principal remaining limitation: a helical core that sits 4.5~\AA{} from the crystal
in a bilayer against 2.6~\AA{} in a detergent micelle. Restoring the absent terms
moves that deviation by less than 0.1~\AA, inside run-to-run scatter, so we report
the deficit as diagnosed but unexplained, together with the seven candidate causes
we have eliminated by measurement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{The data problem, and two responses}

Machine-learned force fields are powerful exactly where data is abundant. Upside
represents each residue by a few interaction sites, evaluates a learned force field
through pre-tabulated splines, and propagates the chain at a comparatively large
timestep;\cite{Jumper2018a,Jumper2018b} trained against the large soluble-protein
record, it delivers folding and conformational thermodynamics at low cost. Membrane
proteins are the opposite regime: experimental structures and stabilities are
comparatively few, and the bilayer is an active thermodynamic participant that a
protein-only model does not contain.

There are two ways to extend a protein model into the membrane. The first is to
\emph{learn} an implicit membrane term from membrane-protein data---an approach a
previous effort in our group pursued, training an implicit-membrane potential
against on the order of forty membrane proteins. Such a model is only as broad as
its training set: forty proteins is a thin basis for a universal membrane-protein
potential, and a learned implicit term risks encoding the idiosyncrasies of the
training proteins rather than transferable membrane physics.

The second route---ours---adds \emph{no} membrane-protein training data. We keep the
machine-learned protein force field where it is strong (protein internal energetics,
learned from abundant soluble data) and supply the membrane from a physical,
transferable lipid model, dry-MARTINI,\cite{Arnarez2015} whose parameters come from
lipid thermodynamics rather than protein structures. Protein and membrane are
coupled through the \emph{raw} dry-MARTINI cross-interactions, with a single
geometric projection to reconcile the one genuine resolution mismatch (multi-bead
sidechain versus single Upside site). Nothing in the protein model, the membrane
model, or their coupling is fit to membrane-protein data. The power of the
combination is that it composes a data-rich learned model with a transferable
physical one, so it can be applied where the \emph{combined} system is data-poor.

\subsection{What must be true, and what we claim}

For this composition to be useful it must be (i) faithful in the domain it is used
for, (ii) universal---one force field for all proteins, no per-system tuning---and
(iii) affordable at Upside's coarse step. We are deliberately precise about (i): the
hybrid is intended as a \emph{thermodynamic} instrument, not a source of absolute
membrane-coupled rates. That intent is not itself validation.

A composed model has a failure mode that a monolithic one does not, and it organises
this paper: the composition can be \emph{incomplete}. Replacing part of a trained
force field with an explicit physical environment silently removes whatever the
replaced term also did. Section~\ref{sec:audit} audits the composed Hamiltonian term
by term and finds exactly this, and Section~\ref{sec:fidelity} measures the price.

The contributions are: (1) a full-resolution dry-MARTINI membrane inside Upside with
every pair interaction served from a spline table that provably equals the published
functional form (Sec.~\ref{sec:membrane}); (2) a shared g-JF force update with one
stochastic bath per real degree of freedom (Sec.~\ref{sec:langevin}); (3) an
\emph{exact} derived-site protein--membrane interface, with measured force closure
(Secs.~\ref{sec:interface},~\ref{sec:exactness}); (4) an audit of what the
composition replaces and what it omits, with the measured structural consequence
(Secs.~\ref{sec:audit},~\ref{sec:fidelity}); and (5) HDX and protein--nanoparticle
applications together with the numerical and analysis pitfalls encountered
(Secs.~\ref{sec:hdx}--\ref{sec:np}), summarised as an explicit evidence boundary
(Table~\ref{tab:evidence}).

\section{Theory and Methods}

\subsection{Units, timestep, and per-step decomposition}
\label{sec:units}

Upside uses reduced units: energy $E_\mathrm{up}=2.9150$~kJ/mol, length in
\AA{}ngstr\"om, mass $m_\mathrm{up}=12$~g/mol, and temperature
$1.0\,T_\mathrm{up}=350.59$~K. Protein and lipids share one numerical step,
$dt=0.009\,t_\mathrm{up}$. Each physical particle is advanced once per step; there is
no lipid substep and no displacement cap. There is a single system temperature $T$,
passed both to the Upside thermostat and to the friction calibration, so the $k_BT$
in the Einstein relation is the $k_BT$ in the random impulse. All native dry-MARTINI
parameters (nm, kJ/mol, $e$) are converted to Upside units once, at spline-table
build time; no conversion constants are baked into force-field artifacts and the
engine performs no unit conversion at run time.

At each step all real degrees of freedom advance once under a single g-JF force
schedule (Eq.~\ref{eq:gjf}). Environment particles and membrane-contacting protein
carriers receive the production frictions of Sec.~\ref{sec:timescale}; zero-contact
carriers retain the standard Ornstein--Uhlenbeck bath, and positive-friction
particles are excluded from it. Derived sites---the regenerated carbonyl~O and the
backbone centre---are not degrees of freedom and receive neither update nor
thermostat. Preparation damping is deliberately separate from production
calibration: softened early stages use native dry-MARTINI damping and full-core
settling uses strong damping to dissipate the energy released as packing overlaps
resolve. Those stages prepare coordinates; their rate of motion has no physical
meaning.

The mechanical unit implied by $E_\mathrm{up}$, \AA, and $m_\mathrm{up}$ is
$0.20290$~ps, used only for unit conversion. The declared protein clock is 40~ps per
step, and the factor-four MARTINI correction enters exactly once, in the mobility
calibration below. This declared clock is a calibration convention, not evidence
that a bonded lipid evolves on physical time.

\subsection{The full-resolution dry-MARTINI membrane in Upside}
\label{sec:membrane}

Each lipid is represented by its full complement of dry-MARTINI beads, with
intramolecular connectivity taken from the dry-MARTINI topology---harmonic bonds
(equilibrium lengths $0.37$--$0.48$~nm, force constant
$1250$~kJ\,mol$^{-1}$nm$^{-2}$) and harmonic angles ($25$--$45$
kJ\,mol$^{-1}$rad$^{-2}$)---as Upside \texttt{dist\_spring} and
\texttt{angle\_spring} nodes. We use the full-resolution lipid rather than a further
reduced one because a single-particle lipid cannot simultaneously reproduce packing,
hydrophobic thickness, and the orientational response that sets transmembrane tilt;
an earlier single-particle model failed there and was abandoned.

Nonbonded interactions are not evaluated analytically at run time. Following
Upside's convention, every pair interaction is written to an HDF5 spline table ahead
of the run and read through one \texttt{martini\_potential} node with a cell-list
neighbour search. A spline table in this framework is a \emph{representation, never
a variant}: evaluated in native units it must equal the published dry-MARTINI form
exactly, including how that form reaches its cutoff. Concretely, the tabulated
energy is potential-shifted Lennard-Jones plus reaction-field electrostatics with
$\epsilon_r=15$ and $\epsilon_\mathrm{rf}=0$, both vanishing at 1.2~nm. We assert
this equivalence numerically at build time rather than assuming it: the tabulated
grid agrees with the analytic expression to a scale-aware tolerance of $10^{-10}$.
An earlier version of the table truncated the Coulomb term instead of applying the
reaction field, and separately floored the radial argument at $0.1\sigma$; both are
corrected here, and the floor in particular is discussed as a numerical failure mode
in Sec.~\ref{sec:numerics}.

The hard $r^{-12}$ core is kept as parameterized---no capping, no softening---which
is essential for bilayer packing and mechanics and for confining an embedded protein
without a restraint.

\subsection{Single-step g-JF Langevin dynamics}
\label{sec:langevin}

Each bead is advanced by one Gr\o{}nbech-Jensen--Farago Langevin
step.\cite{GronbechJensen2013} For bead $i$ of mass $m_i$, absolute friction
$\alpha_i$, and force $\mathbf{f}=-\nabla_i U$,
\begin{align}
b_i &= \Big(1+\tfrac{\alpha_i dt}{2m_i}\Big)^{-1}, \quad
a_i = \Big(1-\tfrac{\alpha_i dt}{2m_i}\Big) b_i, \\
\mathbf{x}_i^{\,n+1} &= \mathbf{x}_i^{\,n} + b_i dt\,\mathbf{v}_i^{\,n}
  + \tfrac{b_i dt^2}{2m_i}\mathbf{f}_i^{\,n}
  + \tfrac{b_i dt}{2m_i}\boldsymbol{\beta}_i^{\,n+1}, \\
\mathbf{v}_i^{\,n+1} &= a_i\mathbf{v}_i^{\,n}
  + \tfrac{dt}{2m_i}\big(a_i\mathbf{f}_i^{\,n}+\mathbf{f}_i^{\,n+1}\big)
  + \tfrac{b_i}{m_i}\boldsymbol{\beta}_i^{\,n+1},
\label{eq:gjf}
\end{align}
with $\boldsymbol{\beta}_i^{\,n+1}=\sqrt{2\alpha_i k_BT\,dt}\,\boldsymbol{\xi}$ the
discrete thermal impulse. g-JF has exact configurational statistics for a harmonic
oscillator within the Verlet stability limit, not unconditional exactness for an
arbitrary nonlinear force field; static results therefore require timestep-halving
and distributional convergence checks.

\subsection{Why molecular diffusion does not calibrate a one-step run}
\label{sec:designspace}

The preferred kinetic target is whole-molecule lateral diffusion,
$D_\mathrm{DOPC}=11.5~\mu\mathrm{m}^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ after the MARTINI
factor-four correction. At 40~ps of declared time per step this is a
molecular-COM increment of
$\langle \Delta r_{xy}^{2}\rangle = 4D_\mathrm{DOPC}(40~\mathrm{ps})=0.184~\text{\AA}^2$
per step. A one-step scan cannot reproduce it. Reducing friction to a free-draining
molecular estimate and nearly to zero raises fitted molecular diffusion only to
$\approx0.175~\mu\mathrm{m}^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$, while the low-friction interface
heats and loses hydrogen bonds: the motion is inertial and collision-limited rather
than in the overdamped Einstein regime a scalar-friction calibration assumes.

The opposite error is instructive. A rejected mapping used $\alpha=1666.67$ for a
mass-6 bead, giving $\alpha dt/(2m)=1.25$; its momenta appeared thermal while
drift-removed lipid centres of mass moved only $0.17$--$0.24$~\AA{} over the nominal
trajectory. \emph{Momentum temperature does not validate coordinate mobility.}

\subsection{Bare-particle mobility and contact-local protein friction}
\label{sec:timescale}

Production instead calibrates a \emph{bare}, unbonded particle mobility. With
$f_\mathrm{CG}=4$, $\Delta t_\mathrm{protein}=40$~ps, and
$D_\mathrm{target}=11.5~\mu\mathrm{m}^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$, the raw quantities are
$\Delta t_\mathrm{raw}=10$~ps and
$D_\mathrm{raw}=46~\mu\mathrm{m}^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$; the factor appears once, and
$D_\mathrm{raw}\Delta t_\mathrm{raw}=D_\mathrm{target}\Delta t_\mathrm{protein}$.
Matching the mean-square displacement accumulated over one numerical step,
\begin{equation}
  D_\mathrm{bead,up}
  =\frac{D_\mathrm{raw}(10^{-4}~\text{\AA}^2/\mathrm{ps})\Delta t_\mathrm{raw}}
  {dt_\mathrm{up}} = 5.11111~\text{\AA}^2/t_\mathrm{up},
  \label{eq:bead_diffusion}
\end{equation}
and the Einstein relation sets $\alpha_\mathrm{bead}=k_BT/D_\mathrm{bead,up}$. Every
environment particle receives $\alpha_\mathrm{bead}$. For a protein backbone carrier
$i$ the model counts environment beads within the 12~\AA{} interaction range and
assigns $\alpha_i=n_i\alpha_\mathrm{bead}$, recomputed at stage handoff and held
fixed within a segment so that position-dependent multiplicative noise is not
introduced silently.

This assumes independent additive bead drag, omitting friction correlations,
anisotropy, hydrodynamic coupling, and memory. It is a reproducible local damping
model, not a measurement of the friction a real membrane protein experiences, and a
bead bonded into a lipid shares that molecule's long-time diffusion, so trajectories
must still report molecular diffusion independently.

\subsection{Membrane mechanics from a Monte-Carlo barostat}
\label{sec:mechanics}

The area compressibility requires the equilibrium area \emph{fluctuations},
$K_A=k_BT\langle A\rangle/\mathrm{Var}(A)$. A weak-coupling barostat relaxes the box
but suppresses its fluctuations and cannot yield $K_A$. We therefore use a
Monte-Carlo barostat: trial box rescalings are applied by scaling lipid centres of
mass, preserving internal geometry so that $\Delta U$ reflects area elasticity
rather than bond stiffness, and accepted with the exact constant-pressure Metropolis
weight $\exp[-\beta(\Delta U+P\Delta V)](V'/V)^{N_\mathrm{mol}}$. Being a
Monte-Carlo move it samples the isothermal--isobaric ensemble by construction,
independent of any coupling constant.

\subsection{The protein--membrane interface}
\label{sec:interface}

The protein retains its native Upside force field, whose persistent backbone
coordinates are N, C$\alpha$, and C. Upside already regenerates the carbonyl O and
amide H as derived sites through its \texttt{infer\_H\_O} node. The dry-MARTINI
backbone bead is likewise derived. For residue $k$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf r_\mathrm{BB}^{(k)}=\frac{
  14\,\mathbf r_\mathrm{N}^{(k)}+12\,\mathbf r_{\mathrm{C}\alpha}^{(k)}
  +12\,\mathbf r_\mathrm{C}^{(k)}+16\,\mathbf r_\mathrm{O}^{(k)}}{54},
  \label{eq:bb_forward}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf r_\mathrm{O}^{(k)}$ is taken \emph{from} \texttt{infer\_H\_O} rather
than reconstructed from a stored reference frame. This choice is what makes the
interface exact. Because Eq.~\ref{eq:bb_forward} is then a linear combination of
nodes the engine already differentiates, the reverse pass is a constant-weight
split: the N, C$\alpha$, and C shares are added to the integrated coordinates, and
the $16/54$ O share is handed to \texttt{infer\_H\_O}, whose own chain rule carries
it back to C$\alpha$, C, and the following residue's N---which is where the frame's
rotational dependence lives. No placement Jacobian is required and no sensitivity is
discarded. The weights sum to unity, so
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{a\in\{\mathrm{N},\mathrm{C}\alpha,\mathrm{C}\}}\!\!\!w_a
  \;+\;w_\mathrm{O}=1,
  \qquad
  \Delta\mathbf s_a = w_a\,\mathbf s_\mathrm{BB},
  \label{eq:bb_reverse}
\end{equation}
and Newton's third law holds by construction. This supersedes an earlier partial
route in which the O share was dropped and only $38/54$ of a backbone sensitivity
reached persistent coordinates; that construction was non-conservative and is
measured against here in Sec.~\ref{sec:exactness}.

Two implementation details matter. First, the environment side is not special-cased:
the node that serves the pair table is a pass-through copy of the coordinate array
with only the derived slots overwritten, so an environment bead's sensitivity returns
to its own integrated coordinate unchanged. Second, the C-terminal residue has no
\texttt{infer\_H\_O} acceptor---the node builds a carbonyl O from the \emph{next}
residue's N, and emits $n_\mathrm{res}-1$ acceptors---so that one backbone centre is
the N/C$\alpha$/C mass centre with the weights renormalised. This is not a numerical
guard; the site genuinely does not exist.

\paragraph{SC--environment.} Upside resolves a sidechain into one directional site,
dry-MARTINI into several beads. This one genuine resolution mismatch is bridged by a
potential of mean force: for each residue type the multi-bead sidechain is projected
onto the single Upside site by a Boltzmann average over eliminated conformations,
\begin{equation}
U_\mathrm{SC\text{-}env}(\mathbf{r}) = -k_BT\,\ln\Big\langle
  \exp[-U_\mathrm{MARTINI}(\mathbf{r};\Omega)/k_BT]\Big\rangle_{\Omega},
\end{equation}
giving a smooth, orientation-resolved spline table. This is the only PMF in the
interface.

\paragraph{BB--environment.} The backbone is a single site in both models, so nothing
is integrated out: BB--env is the raw dry-MARTINI interaction from the same spline as
lipid--lipid. No softening, no PMF, no capping.

\paragraph{Confinement.} No term keeps the protein in the membrane. Its equilibrium
depth and orientation follow from the raw force field, as they should.

\subsection{What the composition replaces, and what it omits}
\label{sec:audit}

Composing two force fields requires knowing which terms of the parent model the
new environment \emph{replaces} and which it merely \emph{removes}. We audited the
composed Hamiltonian against a standard Upside configuration of the same protein.

The replacement is deliberate and correct. In standard Upside the rotamer solver
takes its one-body field from an implicit-solvent environment coupling; in the
hybrid it takes \texttt{martini\_sc\_table\_1body}, the explicit lipid field. Upside's
implicit \emph{bilayer} term is likewise, and correctly, absent: an explicit-lipid
model must not also carry an implicit membrane slab.

The omission is not deliberate. Three groups of \emph{protein--protein} terms present
in every standard Upside configuration have no MARTINI counterpart, because MARTINI
supplies only protein--environment interactions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the many-body protein self-burial coupling, which rewards a residue for being
  buried by \emph{other protein residues} and is the principal driver of tertiary
  helix packing;
  \item the corresponding backbone burial coupling; and
  \item the sidechain-to-backbone hydrogen-bond coverage terms, which in standard
  Upside are solved self-consistently \emph{inside} the rotamer node and make a
  buried hydrogen bond stronger than an exposed one.
\end{enumerate}
The explicit lipid field rewards protein--lipid contact; nothing in the composed
model rewards the protein for packing against itself beyond pairwise rotamer
interactions, and nothing modulates backbone hydrogen-bond strength by burial.
Section~\ref{sec:fidelity} measures what this costs. We regard this as the central
methodological lesson of the work: \emph{a composed force field must be audited term
by term, because substituting an environment silently removes whatever else the
substituted term was doing.}

\subsection{A frame-origin error in the sidechain anchor}
\label{sec:cbfix}

Auditing the composed model against a standard Upside configuration of the same
protein also exposed an implementation error worth recording, because its signature
is easy to miss. The residue frame produced by \texttt{affine\_alignment} has its
origin at the \emph{centroid} of N, C$\alpha$, and C---the three atoms are centred
before the Kabsch fit, and the stored reference geometry is centred to match---so a
site placed in that frame must be specified relative to the centroid. The hybrid
preparation stored the raw C$\beta$ coordinate instead, displacing every sidechain
anchor by exactly the centroid, $0.568$~\AA. Since \texttt{martini\_sc\_table\_1body}
takes that anchor as its sidechain input, the error biased every
sidechain--environment energy and force by roughly 12\% of a bead radius; the total
potential of an identical configuration moves by $\approx680~E_\mathrm{up}$ when it
is corrected. The spline tables are unaffected, because they are functions of
distance and angle \emph{relative} to the anchor.

The error was found by differencing the two configurations array by array. What made
it visible was that the bonded and hydrogen-bonding core came back bit-identical
(maximum difference zero for the Ramachandran maps, hydrogen-bond parameters, and
backbone pair terms), so the two arrays that did differ could not be dismissed as
index remapping. We note this as a practical argument for differencing a composed
model against a reference configuration of its parent, rather than only testing that
it runs.

\subsection{Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

All pre-production stages treat the persistent backbone coordinates of the complete
protein as one rigid body: forces may translate and rotate it while a mass-weighted
rigid projection prevents internal deformation. This rule remains active through a
dedicated handoff stage in which the final, unsoftened BB--env and SC--env
interactions are already active, so interface clashes are removed by moving the
membrane and the protein as physical bodies rather than by unfolding the protein. A
rigid burn-in then lets the bilayer relax around the fully coupled protein. Only at
an explicit transition is the rigid group cleared. There is no positional spring,
staged release, interaction softening, or gap in interface activation at that
transition, so pre-production protects the input fold without adding any restraint
to the production Hamiltonian.

Temperature replica exchange is supported natively, with temperatures pinned to slots
so each slot is canonical; the lipid potential enters the exchange criterion.
Systems reported here are a pure DOPC bilayer, transmembrane proteins 1RKL (Ost4p)
and 1AFO (glycophorin~A dimer), \emph{E.~coli} GlpG in both a dodecyl-maltoside
micelle and a POPE/POPG bilayer, and human serum albumin on a functionalised gold
nanoparticle.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Exactness of the interface force route}
\label{sec:exactness}

The derived-site construction of Sec.~\ref{sec:interface} is exact, and we measure
it rather than assert it. On a folded test protein the force remaining on the
derived backbone-centre slots and on the derived O slots is identically
\textbf{zero}, confirming that those sites carry no spurious dynamics, and the total
force over all particles sums to $1.7\times10^{-9}$ of the largest single force,
confirming that the split returns exactly what the pair table deposited. A
20{,}000-step 1RKL trajectory on this route shows no non-finite values, potential
relaxing from $-18{,}173$ to $-19{,}810~E_\mathrm{up}$, radius of gyration
$12.76\rightarrow12.43$~\AA, worst peptide C--N distance 1.53~\AA{} with none above
2~\AA, and $\langle E_\mathrm{kin}\rangle/\tfrac32k_BT=1.013$.

This is a qualitative change from the superseded partial route, under which more
force was discarded than delivered and a one-sided residual acted on the environment
every step. That construction did net work regardless of step size and produced
blow-ups localised on individual residues; the present route removes that failure
mode by construction.

\subsection{Baseline bilayer structure, thermostat, and transport}
\label{sec:structure}

The baseline bilayer remains assembled with area per lipid $\approx65$~\AA$^2$
(experiment $\approx67$), hydrophobic thickness $\approx2.78$~nm (experiment
$2.7$--$2.9$), chain order parameter $\approx0.30$, and a fluid director
distribution. For the POPE/POPG bilayer used in the GlpG work, the measured
phosphate--phosphate thickness is $38.0\pm0.1$~\AA{} with a $25.4$~\AA{} acyl core,
i.e.\ a hydrophobic mismatch of only $-2.8$~\AA{} against GlpG's belt---the bilayer
geometry is not a source of error. Protein and lipid kinetic-energy ratios are
$1.001$--$1.012$ times $\tfrac32k_BT$, verifying that the stochastic update
thermalizes momenta at the temperature it is given. Drift-removed molecular
diffusion remains $10^{-3}$--$10^{-2}~\mu\mathrm{m}^2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ against the
$11.5$ target: a clean diffusive regime has not been reached and molecular diffusion
remains uncalibrated.

\subsection{Structural stability and the limits of a stability test}
\label{sec:hybrid}

The raw spline interface keeps both test proteins embedded without a confining
potential, and the rigid handoff preserves the fold it is given: the maximum change
among all pairwise backbone-carrier distances between the last pre-production
structure and the production input is 0.017~\AA{} (1RKL) and 0.0037~\AA{} (1AFO).
For 1RKL, DSSP assigns 23 helical residues at both the first and last of 1{,}001
production frames (minimum 16, mean 21.9), and every residue of the central
transmembrane segment 10--28 is helical in 96.5--100\% of frames; for 1AFO the
first/final/minimum/mean counts are 54/51/48/50.8.

This validates the force-routing and preparation construct. It does \emph{not}
validate the equilibrium probability of helix opening, and Sec.~\ref{sec:fidelity}
shows why a stability test is the weaker claim.

\subsection{Fold fidelity, and the price of the omitted terms}
\label{sec:fidelity}

We propagated GlpG in two environments that differ only in the explicit
environment---a POPE/POPG bilayer and a dodecyl-maltoside micelle---using the
identical protein force field and interface, and compared both against the crystal
structure (Table~\ref{tab:fidelity}).

The helical core sits 4.15~\AA{} from the crystal in the bilayer, and this is an
equilibrium property rather than ongoing drift: it rises within the first segment
and then holds across two further segments. Loosened packing lowers protein
self-burial, so the structural protection criteria fail together in 3.3\% of frames
and the implied local opening free energy is $\approx2$~kcal/mol.

We eliminated the obvious alternatives by measurement. It is not the integrator: the
kinetic-energy excess is $+2$--$3\%$ and timestep-independent, covalent geometry is
intact, the temperature dependence is weak, and the same integrator gives 2.61~\AA{}
in the micelle. It is not the hydrogen-bond definition: on the crystal geometry the
Upside score agrees with the DSSP electrostatic criterion to within 8\% inside
helices, and the disagreements are marginal. It is not lipid packing: every backbone
site has environment beads within 8~\AA{} (nearest-neighbour distance
$5.2$~\AA{} median), so there is no annular void. It is not hydrophobic mismatch, as
noted above.

We also tested the omission identified in Sec.~\ref{sec:audit} directly, and it is
\emph{not} the explanation either. Three trajectories were propagated from one
identical configuration: the model as composed, the model with the many-body
protein self-burial term restored, and the model with every non-membrane Upside term
restored---the last differing from a standard configuration by a single node. At
matched length (750--780~k steps) the helical-core deviations were
$4.61\pm0.09$, $4.71\pm0.12$, and $4.53\pm0.08$~\AA{} respectively, i.e.\ changes
of $+0.10$ and $-0.08$~\AA{} against a target of roughly $-1.5$~\AA. The radius of
gyration remained 20.2--20.4~\AA{} in all three, so the anticipated failure mode---an
implicit-solvent burial term over-burying the lipid-facing surface---did not occur;
the terms simply have little leverage on this observable. Separately, correcting the
sidechain-anchor displacement of Sec.~\ref{sec:cbfix} did not improve fidelity
either.

We therefore report the deficit as diagnosed but unexplained. Seven candidate causes
have been eliminated by measurement---integrator, hydrogen-bond assignment, lipid
packing, hydrophobic mismatch, the protection threshold, the sidechain anchor, and
the absent protein--protein terms. The one node-level difference from a standard
configuration that remains untested is the rotamer one-body representation: the
hybrid uses fixed rotamer probabilities where standard Upside uses rama-dependent
ones. Whatever the cause, it, and not the interface mechanics, is what currently
bounds quantitative membrane-protein thermodynamics with this model.

\subsection{Numerical failure modes}
\label{sec:numerics}

Two failure modes are worth recording because both are silent.

First, an early spline table floored the radial argument at $0.1\sigma$, producing a
constant potential---and therefore \emph{exactly zero force}---inside
$0.47$--$0.60$~\AA. A pair entering that region felt no restoring force and stayed.
Removing the floor and setting the inner knot at $0.3$~\AA{} restores the published
form, but a clamped spline still flattens below its inner knot and that domain is
still occasionally entered.

Second, and more instructive, the resulting blow-ups do not originate there.
Instrumenting the pair loop through a complete event shows the cascade beginning with
two \emph{environment} beads at 1.83~\AA---well inside the valid table domain, where
the tabulated force is correct and simply enormous ($3.4\times10^{6}$)---and reaching
sub-0.3~\AA{} separations only 1{,}072 force evaluations later. The zero-force
plateau is the sink the cascade ends in, not its source. We note two diagnostic
traps: after the event every force is NaN, so accumulators that compare against a
running maximum silently report zero; and \texttt{isfinite} remains true for
\emph{96 frames} after the system is destroyed, as does the peptide-bond count, since
the damage is in the lipids. The discriminating test is the sign of the total
potential---a condensed bilayer plus protein sits near $-2.2\times10^{4}~E_\mathrm{up}$,
so a positive total means core overlap.

\subsection{Application: hydrogen--deuterium exchange}
\label{sec:hdx}

In the EX2 regime the observed rate factorizes as
$k_\mathrm{HDX}=K_\mathrm{op}k_\mathrm{chem}
=e^{-\Delta G_\mathrm{op}/k_BT}k_\mathrm{chem}$, so opening and closing rates enter
only through their equilibrium ratio and the MD time axis does not appear. The
lipid-clock mismatch therefore does not rescale reported HDX seconds; it matters
indirectly, because slow dynamics can leave the protected population biased.

Two analysis defects encountered here generalise to any hybrid trajectory. First,
MBAR must be given reduced potentials referenced to a common constant: a coupled
protein-plus-membrane potential is $O(10^4)~E_\mathrm{up}$, $\exp(-u)$ overflows, and
the solver never leaves its initial guess---returning $f_k=0$, uniform weights, and
an unweighted average over the whole ladder \emph{that looks like a reweighted
ensemble}. Subtracting one pooled-mean constant is an exact transformation and fixes
it; on our ladder it moves the free-energy spread from $0.000$ to $106.9$ and the
neighbour overlap from $0$ to $0.09$--$0.27$. Second, a protection-to-$\Delta G$
converter that clips the protected probability at $0.99999$ imposes a hard ceiling of
$0.001987\,T_\mathrm{scale}\ln(10^5)=6.8$~kcal/mol and diverts the most protected
amides into a sentinel; on identical frames, removing the clip raises the maximum
from 6.3 to 19.6~kcal/mol and resolves every residue.

With both corrected, the POPE/POPG wildtype ladder (16 replicas, 184{,}528 pooled
frames) resolves 187 of 203 amides at the reference temperature with
$\Delta G$ spanning $-2.4$ to $+18.6$~kcal/mol and the expected six-band structure
along the sequence. Consistent with Sec.~\ref{sec:fidelity}, the baseline is lower
than an experimental profile would suggest, and we attribute that to fold fidelity
rather than to sampling: all off-scale amides fall inside DSSP helices and none in
loops, and $\Delta G$ correlates with both H-bond occupancy ($r=0.59$) and burial
($r=0.65$).

\subsection{A lipid-contact accessibility term}
\label{sec:hdx-access}

A membrane protein needs one further ingredient: an open amide must also be
water-accessible. The maintained protection model scores an amide as protected when
it is intramolecularly hydrogen bonded or buried by protein, with no lipid term, so a
transmembrane amide facing the bilayer---shielded from water by lipid but not buried
by protein---leaves the protected state whenever its hydrogen bond momentarily
breaks, fragmenting the continuous protection expected of a non-exchanging helix.

We recover the missing term from the coupled trajectory, which retains the explicit
lipid. Per frame, a backbone nitrogen is marked water-inaccessible when at least a
threshold number of lipid tail beads lie within a fixed cutoff, and the resulting
$a\in\{0,1\}$ gates the exchange-competent population. This is the explicit-lipid
analogue of the implicit-membrane protection term, differing only in how ``facing
lipid'' is measured. The cutoff and count are physical but first-order, and should be
recalibrated against explicit-water MARTINI or all-atom reference.

Applied to catalytically inactivated GlpG in a DDM belt, the transmembrane helices
recover continuous protection and one helix remains conspicuously mobile:
transmembrane helix~4, which carries the catalytic serine at its amino terminus. TM4
is a short helix that does not span the bilayer and is flanked by conserved
helix-breaking glycines,\cite{Wang2006,BenShem2007} and the active-site network it
organises is energetically weak;\cite{Gaffney2019} rhomboids are also measurably less
stable in detergent than in a bilayer.\cite{Barniol2018,Sawczyc2024} The canonical
TM5/L5 gate\cite{Shi2019,Bohg2023} remained comparatively ordered, so the mobility
localises to the intrinsically irregular catalytic helix rather than the functional
gate.

\subsection{Application: a protein on a nanoparticle}
\label{sec:np}

The same composition principle applies to any data-poor combination of
well-parameterized parts. We propagated human serum albumin on a 5~nm
mercaptopropionic-acid-functionalised gold nanoparticle and computed a per-residue
footprint, testing a published lysine-footprinting claim that a core lysine is
favoured by particle binding.

The result illustrates a general hazard in interpreting such simulations. Albumin
does not merely deform on the particle; it unravels, with $R_g$ reaching
$39$--$172$~\AA{} across six starting orientations. Once spread, contact ceases to
discriminate: core and surface residues show equal mean contact (0.272 versus 0.261).
Restricted to adsorbed-and-compact frames---the state the labelling experiment
probes, and only 3.2\% of sampled frames---the measure separates properly (core 0.128
versus surface 0.245), the claimed K525 and K541 are strongly contacting (0.55 and
0.69), and the primary claimed residue makes \emph{zero} contact while the protein is
still compact. We report this as inconclusive rather than contradictory: 71\% of the
compact window comes from a single orientation, and two orientations exceed the
periodic box in their extended state. The methodological point stands---an
interpretation window must be chosen from the physics of the measurement being
compared to, before looking at the answer.

\section{Discussion}

Three validation layers must remain separate. \emph{Implementation} validation asks
whether tables, force routing, derived coordinates, stochastic baths, and stage
constraints implement the stated equations; the interface now passes this layer in
the strong sense that its force closure is exact and measured. \emph{Configurational}
validation asks whether the finite-timestep simulation samples the intended
distribution and whether that distribution matches the physical system; this layer is
open, and Sec.~\ref{sec:fidelity} identifies a specific, diagnosable reason it is
open. \emph{Kinetic} validation asks whether trajectory time corresponds to molecular
time; the diffusion mismatch fails this layer outright.

The distinction matters because the three are easily conflated. A trajectory that
remains folded for 1{,}001 frames passes an implementation test and says almost
nothing about layer two---as the bilayer-versus-micelle comparison shows, the same
implementation gives 4.15~\AA{} and 2.61~\AA{} core deviations in two environments,
and only the second is within the fidelity one would want before assigning
thermodynamics.

The broader lesson concerns composition itself. The appeal of composing a data-rich
learned model with a transferable physical one is that neither has to be retrained.
The hazard, which we did not anticipate and which cost the most, is that the seam is
not only where the two models \emph{meet} but also where terms of the parent model
quietly \emph{disappear}. Substituting an explicit environment for an implicit one
removes the implicit term's other duties---here, protein self-burial and
burial-dependent hydrogen bonding---because the substitute describes only
protein--environment physics. We would now regard a term-by-term audit of the composed
Hamiltonian, against a standard configuration of the same protein, as a mandatory
step in any such construction rather than a diagnostic of last resort.

Correcting a sampling defect by softening or disabling the physical interface would
change the model and is not acceptable; the same applies to widening a detection
threshold so that a run proceeds.

\section{Conclusions}

A membrane protein and a full-resolution dry-MARTINI bilayer can be propagated
together through one g-JF force schedule with one temperature and one stochastic bath
per real degree of freedom, coupled by raw cross-interactions with no
membrane-protein training data. Making the dry-MARTINI backbone bead a derived site
built from Upside's own regenerated carbonyl O turns the interface from a partial
force route into an exact one: the reverse pass is a constant-weight split, the O
share is propagated by the node that owns it, residual forces on derived sites are
zero, and total force closes to $1.7\times10^{-9}$. Every pair interaction is served
from a spline table proven equal to the published functional form, and the protein is
held rigid until an explicit transition to flexible production, so secondary
structure is preserved without softening or disabling any conservative interaction.

The remaining barrier is not the interface but the composition. An audit of the
composed Hamiltonian shows that the explicit lipid correctly replaces Upside's
sidechain environment field, and correctly excludes its implicit bilayer, but leaves
three protein--protein terms without a counterpart. The measured cost is a helical
core 4.15~\AA{} from the crystal in a bilayer against 2.61~\AA{} in a micelle, an
amide protection deficit that follows from it, and HDX free energies whose shape is
informative while their baseline is not yet quantitative. Restoring those terms,
without double-counting the explicit lipid, is the concrete next step and the
condition under which this hybrid becomes a quantitative membrane-protein
thermodynamic instrument.
