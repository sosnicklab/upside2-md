\title{A Hybrid Machine-Learned/Physical Model for Membrane-Protein
Thermodynamics: Coupling the Upside Protein Force Field to a Full-Resolution
dry-MARTINI Membrane}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Membrane proteins are the archetype of a data-sparse regime: structures and
thermodynamic measurements are scarce relative to soluble proteins, so
data-hungry models are hard to train directly on them. We take the opposite
route from training a membrane-protein-specific potential. We couple two
components that are each already well determined on their own footing---a
\emph{machine-learned} residue-level protein force field (Upside), trained on the
abundant soluble-protein record, and a \emph{physical}, transferable
implicit-solvent lipid force field (dry-MARTINI), parameterized from lipid
physics---through their raw, unmodified cross-interactions. Because neither the
protein model, the membrane model, nor their coupling is fit to
membrane-protein data, the hybrid reaches systems for which little combined data
exists. The technical obstacle is a timescale mismatch: a hard-core dry-MARTINI
bilayer is numerically stable only to tens of femtoseconds, far below Upside's
coarse step. We resolve it by advancing every lipid bead with a single
Gr\o{}nbech-Jensen--Farago (g-JF) inertial-Langevin step, which is
unconditionally stable at the coarse step and---critically---samples the
\emph{correct Boltzmann distribution} with the excluded-volume core intact, so
bilayer structure and mechanics are those of the force field, not of the
integrator. The protein keeps its native Upside representation and force field
(with a Langevin friction added only to thermostat it in the coupled system) and is
held in the membrane by the raw dry-MARTINI field alone. We are explicit about what the model
does and does not deliver. Its rigorous domain is \emph{thermodynamics}:
validated bilayer structure (area per lipid, hydrophobic thickness, chain order)
and mechanics (area compressibility via a Monte-Carlo barostat), and---for the
protein---equilibrium conformational ensembles and local stabilities. Its lipid
\emph{dynamics} are sub-diffusive and admit no single physical time mapping;
coupled protein--lipid \emph{kinetics} are correspondingly effective, not
physical. We argue this is the right trade for the questions the tool targets:
enhanced-sampling free-energy calculations and, in particular, hydrogen-deuterium
exchange (HDX), whose protection factors report an \emph{equilibrium} opening
constant rather than a rate and therefore fall squarely in the trustworthy domain.
Finally, the same compose-by-physical-coupling principle generalizes beyond lipids
to other data-sparse composite systems, such as nanoparticles.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{The data problem, and two responses}

Machine-learned force fields are powerful exactly where data is abundant. Upside
represents each residue by a few interaction sites, evaluates a learned force
field through pre-tabulated splines, and propagates the chain at a comparatively
large timestep; trained against the large soluble-protein record, it delivers
folding and conformational thermodynamics at low cost. Membrane proteins are the
opposite regime: experimental structures and stabilities are comparatively few,
and the environment (the bilayer) is an active thermodynamic participant that a
protein-only model does not contain.

There are two ways to extend a protein model into the membrane. The first is to
\emph{learn} an implicit membrane term from membrane-protein data---an approach a
previous effort in our group pursued, training an implicit-membrane potential
against on the order of forty membrane proteins. Such a model is only as broad as
its training set: forty proteins is a thin basis for a universal
membrane-protein potential, and a learned implicit term risks encoding the
idiosyncrasies of the training proteins rather than transferable membrane physics.

The second route---ours---adds \emph{no} membrane-protein training data at all.
We keep the machine-learned protein force field where it is strong (protein
internal energetics, learned from abundant soluble data) and supply the membrane
from a \emph{physical}, transferable lipid model (dry-MARTINI), whose parameters
come from lipid thermodynamics, not from protein structures. The protein and the
membrane are then coupled through the \emph{raw} dry-MARTINI cross-interactions,
with a single geometric projection to reconcile the one genuine resolution
mismatch (multi-bead side chain vs.\ single Upside site). Nothing in the
protein model, the membrane model, or their coupling is fit to membrane-protein
data. The power of the combination is precisely that it composes a data-rich
learned model with a transferable physical model, so it can be applied where the
\emph{combined} system has little data---which is the membrane-protein regime, and,
as we note in the outlook, other composite systems such as nanoparticles.

\subsection{What must be true, and what we claim}

For this composition to be useful it must be (i) faithful in the domain it is used
for, (ii) universal (one force field for all proteins; no per-system tuning), and
(iii) affordable at Upside's coarse step. We are deliberately precise about (i):
the hybrid is a \emph{thermodynamic} instrument. It reproduces the equilibrium
ensemble---bilayer structure and mechanics, and the protein's conformational and
local-stability distributions in the membrane---because the underlying dynamics,
whatever their rate, are constructed to sample the correct Boltzmann distribution.
It does \emph{not} reproduce physical lipid or coupled-protein kinetics, and we
show below that it cannot be made to: the coarse-grained lipid is intrinsically
sub-diffusive and no single time factor maps it to physical dynamics. This is not
a defect for the target applications. Free-energy methods (replica exchange,
metadynamics) deliberately distort dynamics to accelerate sampling, so they need
correct statics, not correct kinetics; and hydrogen-deuterium exchange, our
flagship observable, measures an equilibrium quantity (Sec.~\ref{sec:hdx}).

The contributions are: (1) a full-resolution dry-MARTINI membrane inside Upside
with every pair interaction served from a spline table (Sec.~\ref{sec:membrane});
(2) a single-step g-JF lipid integrator that is unconditionally stable at the
coarse step and Boltzmann-correct with the hard core intact
(Sec.~\ref{sec:langevin}); (3) a design-space analysis showing that correct
diffusion, a single coarse step, and a hard core are mutually exclusive, and that
even relaxing the single-step constraint does not recover a clean lipid timescale
(Secs.~\ref{sec:designspace},~\ref{sec:timescale}); (4) a validated
static/thermodynamic characterization of the membrane---structure and mechanics,
the latter via a Monte-Carlo barostat that samples the correct area fluctuations
(Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanics}); and (5) a raw-force-field protein--membrane interface
that confines an embedded protein without any added potential
(Sec.~\ref{sec:interface}), enabling equilibrium and HDX observables
(Sec.~\ref{sec:hdx}).

\section{Theory and Methods}

\subsection{Units, timestep, and per-step decomposition}
\label{sec:units}

Upside uses reduced units: energy $E_\mathrm{up}=2.9150$~kJ/mol, length in
{\AA}ngstr\"om, mass $m_\mathrm{up}=12$~g/mol, and temperature
$1.0\,T_\mathrm{up}=350.59$~K. We use Upside's \emph{standard} step,
$dt=0.009\,T_\mathrm{up}$; protein and lipids share this single outer step, and it
is never enlarged to make the coupled system behave---slowing the step would only
mask instabilities that reappear at the standard step. The calibration reference
state is $t_\mathrm{ref}=0.8647\,T_\mathrm{up}$ ($303$~K), where
$k_BT=0.8647\,E_\mathrm{up}$. All native dry-MARTINI parameters (nm, kJ/mol, $e$)
are converted to Upside units once, at spline-table build time; no conversion
constants are baked into the force-field artifacts, and the C++ engine performs no
unit conversion at run time.

At each outer step both species advance \emph{once} under a \emph{single} g-JF
integrator (Eq.~\ref{eq:gjf}) with a per-bead friction scale, so protein and lipids
share one consistent integrator---a genuinely mixed g-JF(lipid)/Verlet(protein)
coupling would exchange the shared interface force inconsistently and inject energy
at the stiff hard core, destabilizing the run. Both species carry Langevin friction,
but at different scales: lipids at the calibrated value (Sec.~\ref{sec:langevin}),
and the protein at a \emph{larger} per-bead friction that thermostats it locally.
This last point is essential and was learned the hard way. A \emph{frictionless}
protein (friction scale zero, i.e.\ plain velocity Verlet relying only on the weak
global thermostat) is a Hamiltonian body coupled to the noisy g-JF lipid bath across
the stiff interface; it cannot dissipate the finite-step interface energy it
receives, so it heats to $k_BT\!\approx\!1.5$--$2\,E_\mathrm{up}$ (well above the
target $0.86$, while the lipids stay correctly thermostatted) and its secondary
structure melts. Giving the protein its own Langevin dissipation---a per-bead
friction roughly two orders of magnitude larger than the lipid value---restores the
fluctuation--dissipation balance and holds the protein at temperature with its fold
intact. Because Langevin friction is Boltzmann-correct, this thermostat coupling
sets the temperature and the (effective) protein kinetics but does \emph{not} bias
the equilibrium ensemble, so equilibrium observables remain correct
(Sec.~\ref{sec:hdx}). The two species couple through the interface interactions of
Sec.~\ref{sec:interface}, evaluated on the shared spline tables.

\subsection{The full-resolution dry-MARTINI membrane in Upside}
\label{sec:membrane}

Each DOPC lipid is its fourteen dry-MARTINI beads. Intramolecular connectivity is
taken from the dry-MARTINI topology: harmonic bonds (equilibrium lengths
$0.37$--$0.48$~nm, force constant $1250$~kJ\,mol$^{-1}$nm$^{-2}$) and harmonic
angles (force constants $25$--$45$~kJ\,mol$^{-1}$rad$^{-2}$), as Upside
\texttt{dist\_spring} and \texttt{angle\_spring} nodes. We adopt the
full-resolution lipid rather than a further-reduced single-particle lipid because
a reduced lipid cannot simultaneously reproduce packing, hydrophobic thickness,
and the orientational response that sets transmembrane tilt; an earlier
single-particle model (documented separately, now abandoned) failed there.

Nonbonded interactions---dry-MARTINI Lennard-Jones from the
\texttt{[nonbond\_params]} matrix, plus the shifted Coulomb term for charged
head-group beads---are \emph{not} evaluated analytically at run time. Following
Upside's spline-table convention, every pair interaction is written to an HDF5
spline table (\texttt{combined\_energy\_grids}) ahead of the run and read back
through a single \texttt{martini\_potential} node with a cell-list neighbour
search. The hard $r^{-12}$ core is kept exactly as parameterized---no capping, no
softening---which is essential for correct bilayer packing and mechanics, for the
transport analysis of Sec.~\ref{sec:timescale}, and for confining an embedded
protein (Sec.~\ref{sec:interface}).

\subsection{Single-step g-JF Langevin lipid dynamics}
\label{sec:langevin}

At the coarse step a naive inertial integrator cannot resolve the stiff bonds and
$r^{-12}$ core. We advance each lipid bead by one Gr\o{}nbech-Jensen--Farago (g-JF)
Langevin step, constructed to give correct configurational (Boltzmann) statistics
at \emph{any} timestep and to remain stable far beyond the naive limit. For bead
$i$ of mass $m_i$, friction $\alpha_i=\gamma\,s_i$ ($s_i$ a per-bead scale), and
force $\mathbf{f}=-\nabla_i U$,
\begin{align}
b_i &= \Big(1+\tfrac{\alpha_i dt}{2m_i}\Big)^{-1}, \qquad
a_i = \Big(1-\tfrac{\alpha_i dt}{2m_i}\Big) b_i, \\
\mathbf{x}_i^{\,n+1} &= \mathbf{x}_i^{\,n} + b_i dt\,\mathbf{v}_i^{\,n}
  + \tfrac{b_i dt^2}{2m_i}\mathbf{f}_i^{\,n}
  + \tfrac{b_i dt}{2m_i}\boldsymbol{\beta}_i^{\,n+1}, \\
\mathbf{v}_i^{\,n+1} &= a_i\mathbf{v}_i^{\,n}
  + \tfrac{dt}{2m_i}\big(a_i\mathbf{f}_i^{\,n}+\mathbf{f}_i^{\,n+1}\big)
  + \tfrac{b_i}{m_i}\boldsymbol{\beta}_i^{\,n+1},
\label{eq:gjf}
\end{align}
with $\boldsymbol{\beta}_i^{\,n+1}=\sqrt{2\alpha_i k_BT\,dt}\,\boldsymbol{\xi}$ the
discrete thermal impulse ($\boldsymbol{\xi}$ standard Gaussian, the same
$\boldsymbol{\beta}$ in both updates). One force evaluation per outer step supplies
$\mathbf{f}^{\,n+1}$; $\mathbf{f}^{\,n}$ is carried from the previous step. Bead
masses are the dry-MARTINI values. The per-bead scale $s_i$ sets each species'
dissipation: lipids use $s_i=1$ (the calibrated friction), while protein sites use a
much larger $s_i$ so the protein is thermostatted locally (Sec.~\ref{sec:units}); the
limit $s_i=0$ recovers plain velocity Verlet but is \emph{not} used for the protein,
because a frictionless protein coupled to the lipid bath overheats.

The g-JF step buys two things at the coarse step: unconditional stability of the
stiff interactions, and---the property the whole thermodynamic claim rests on---the
\emph{correct static structure}, because g-JF preserves the Boltzmann distribution
independent of $dt$. What it does not preserve is the \emph{rate} of motion; that
limitation is quantified in Sec.~\ref{sec:timescale}.

\subsection{Design space: correct diffusion, single step, hard core---choose two}
\label{sec:designspace}

One might hope to keep a single coarse step \emph{and} exact diffusion \emph{and}
the hard core by tuning friction or softening the core. One cannot. Correct
lateral diffusion fixes the mobility; a single overdamped step then fixes the
per-bead thermal displacement at $\sqrt{2k_BT\,dt/\gamma}\approx2$~\AA{} per
component, of order the bead radius, which drives beads into the $r^{-12}$ wall
(the update is stable only where $\mathrm{mob}\cdot U''<2$, but the wall curvature
gives $\mathrm{mob}\cdot U''\gg2$). Softening the wall enough to remove the
overshoot---for a Beutler soft core, $\alpha\approx1$---drives the contact energy
$4\varepsilon(\alpha^{-2}-\alpha^{-1})\to0$, so beads overlap at no cost and the
excluded volume that maintains area and thickness is destroyed. Since diffusion
and noise share the same mobility, no Markovian single-step integrator can shrink
the kick without shrinking $D$. The three requirements are mutually exclusive.

This leaves two corners. \emph{(i) Relax the single step:} advance the lipids with
$M$ overdamped inner sub-steps small enough to stay below $\sigma$ (a hard-core
bilayer needs $M\approx90$); exact per the mobility, at $M$-fold cost. \emph{(ii)
Relax exact single-step diffusion:} keep the single coarse step and the hard core
with the g-JF integrator, and accept a lipid dynamics that is too slow. We choose
corner~(ii) because it preserves Upside's native protein dynamics and single-step
economy while keeping the membrane force field untouched. Corner~(i) is retained in
the code as a reference. Crucially, though, corner~(i) does \emph{not} rescue a
physical lipid timescale either, for the reason given next.

\subsection{The lipid timescale is effective, not physical}
\label{sec:timescale}

We initially hoped a single ``effective-time factor'' would map the too-slow g-JF
diffusion onto experiment, in the spirit of MARTINI's factor-4 convention. Direct
measurement shows this is not tenable, and the reason is physical rather than a
tuning failure.

\emph{The lipid is sub-diffusive.} On the accessible window the phosphate
mean-squared displacement grows as $\mathrm{MSD}\propto t^{\,\alpha}$ with the
local exponent \emph{falling} from $\alpha\approx0.5$ to $\alpha\approx0.3$ rather
than approaching the Fickian $\alpha=1$; there is no diffusive plateau, so no
single diffusion coefficient exists to be rescaled. Any apparent match of a
rescaled $D$ to experiment is a transient crossing of a falling curve, not a
recovered constant.

\emph{It is a property of the regime, not of the g-JF integrator.} The
exact-diffusion overdamped reference of corner~(i), run in the same box, is
\emph{also} sub-diffusive over the same window (comparable exponent). The
sub-diffusion therefore reflects the small, dense bilayer patch and the
short-to-intermediate-time regime---where real membranes are themselves
sub-diffusive until $\sim$$10$--$100$~ns---rather than the single-step
approximation. A clean Fickian $D$ for \emph{either} integrator would require a
much larger patch and far longer sampling.

\emph{The clock is mode-dependent.} Matching the g-JF director reorientation
autocorrelation to an all-atom CHARMM36 DOPC reference does not overlay with a
single time scaling (the curve \emph{shapes} differ, because the atomistic
director carries fast acyl-tail flexibility the stiff coarse tail lacks), and the
scaling it does favour differs from the diffusion-implied one. Different
observables imply different, incompatible time factors. We therefore report lipid
time as a \emph{nominal} sampling coordinate, not physical time, and make no
quantitative kinetic claim from the membrane clock. \textbf{[EFFECTIVE]}

What \emph{is} robust in the dynamics is the temperature \emph{trend}: across
$T_\mathrm{up}=0.5$--$1.5$ the g-JF bilayer is stable at every temperature and the
apparent diffusion increases monotonically with $T$ in an Arrhenius manner, so
\emph{relative} statements across temperature or condition are reliable even though
the absolute rate is not. This is what makes the model safe to use across a
replica-exchange temperature ladder.

\subsection{Membrane mechanics from a Monte-Carlo barostat}
\label{sec:mechanics}

Area compressibility $K_A$ and, through it, the bending modulus require sampling
the equilibrium area \emph{fluctuations}, $K_A=k_BT\langle A\rangle/\mathrm{Var}(A)$.
A weak-coupling (Berendsen- or damped-piston-style) barostat relaxes the box but
suppresses its fluctuations, so it cannot yield $K_A$. We therefore use a
\emph{Monte-Carlo} barostat: at fixed intervals, trial isotropic-in-plane and
normal box rescalings are applied by scaling lipid \emph{centre-of-mass} positions
(internal geometry preserved, so $\Delta U$ is intermolecular and reflects area
elasticity rather than bond stiffness) and accepted with the exact
constant-pressure Metropolis weight
$\exp\!\big[-\beta(\Delta U+P\Delta V)\big]\,(V'/V)^{N_\mathrm{mol}}$. Being a
Monte-Carlo move, it samples the correct isothermal--isobaric ensemble by
construction, independent of any coupling constant, and restores physical area
fluctuations. Consistent with the sub-diffusive lipid dynamics, the area
decorrelates slowly, so a converged $K_A$ requires long sampling; the barostat is
correct, the convergence is the cost. \textbf{[VALIDATED (method); OPEN (converged
$K_A$)]}

\subsection{The protein--membrane interface}
\label{sec:interface}

The protein keeps its native Upside representation and force field and is advanced
once per outer step; it is not rescaled or placed in any lipid-specific loop. Its
integrator carries the added Langevin friction of Sec.~\ref{sec:units} (a thermostat
coupling, not a force-field change) so it holds temperature against the lipid bath. It
couples to the membrane through the unmodified dry-MARTINI force field.

\paragraph{SC-env (side chain $\leftrightarrow$ lipid).} Upside resolves a side
chain into one directional site, dry-MARTINI into several beads. This one genuine
resolution mismatch is bridged by a potential of mean force: for each residue
type, the multi-bead side chain is projected onto its single Upside site by a
Boltzmann average over the eliminated conformations,
\begin{equation}
U_\mathrm{SC\text{-}env}(\mathbf{r}) = -k_BT\,\ln\Big\langle
  \exp\!\big[-U_\mathrm{MARTINI}(\mathbf{r};\Omega)/k_BT\big]\Big\rangle_{\Omega},
\end{equation}
giving a smooth, orientation-resolved spline table (\texttt{sc\_table}). This is
the only PMF in the interface.

\paragraph{BB-env (backbone $\leftrightarrow$ lipid).} The backbone is a single
site in both models, so nothing is integrated out: BB-env is the \emph{raw}
dry-MARTINI Lennard-Jones (plus Coulomb) interaction, from the same
\texttt{martini\_potential} spline as lipid--lipid. No softening, no PMF, no
capping. An early softened BB-env was tried and found to \emph{cause} the protein
to drift out of the bilayer (Sec.~\ref{sec:hybrid}); the raw field is retained.

\paragraph{Protein confinement.} No term keeps the protein in the membrane; its
equilibrium depth and orientation follow from the raw force field, as they should
from a correct force field rather than a restraint. There is no $z$-restraint and
no implicit-membrane solvation potential.

\subsection{Enhanced sampling and simulation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

Because both the intended observables and the slow, sub-diffusive intrinsic
dynamics point the same way, the model is used with enhanced sampling. Temperature
replica exchange is supported natively (coordinate-swap convention: temperatures
are pinned to slots and the per-slot temperature is applied to the lipid
integrator, so each slot is a canonical ensemble); the lipid potential enters the
exchange criterion, and no momentum rescaling is needed. Well-tempered
metadynamics on a collective variable is available as a native bias node (a
history-dependent Gaussian bias read off a coordinate node, its gradient
propagated to the atoms), depositing on the true simulation-round clock. Both are
equilibrium-sampling methods: they require correct statics, which the model
provides, and they are what converge the rare events (e.g.\ local helix opening)
that the slow intrinsic dynamics would otherwise under-sample.

Systems: a pure DOPC bilayer (structure, mechanics, transport), and transmembrane
proteins 1RKL (Ost4p) and 1AFO (glycophorin~A dimer). Lateral diffusion is
measured from the phosphate in-plane MSD with centre-of-mass drift removed;
protein tilt is measured backbone-only.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Bilayer structure and mechanics (the rigorous domain)}
\label{sec:structure}

Under the single g-JF step the bilayer is stable and fluid, and---because g-JF is
Boltzmann-correct---its structure is that of the force field. Measured against
DOPC: area per lipid $\approx65$~\AA$^2$ (exp.\ $\approx67$), hydrophobic
thickness (first-tail-bead separation) $\approx2.78$~nm (exp.\ $2.7$--$2.9$),
chain order parameter $\approx0.30$ (MARTINI range), and a fluid director
distribution (mean tilt $\approx22^\circ$, order $S\approx0.74$). The
phosphate--phosphate distance reads slightly thick ($\approx43$~\AA{}) but the
excess is head-group projection; the functionally decisive hydrophobic thickness,
to which a transmembrane protein matches, is on target. A zero-tension
Monte-Carlo-barostat run relaxes the area to a somewhat more condensed value than
the fixed-box state, indicating the force field is marginally over-cohesive---a
force-field fidelity note, reported and not tuned away. \textbf{[VALIDATED]}

\subsection{The single-step barrier and the effective clock, demonstrated}
\label{sec:barrier}

The design-space analysis is borne out: at the correct mobility a single overdamped
step drives the raw core into overshoot (energy spikes, disintegration), while the
minimum softening that stabilizes it collapses the excluded volume; the g-JF step
is stable at $M=1$ with the hard core intact and yields the correct structure. The
timescale analysis of Sec.~\ref{sec:timescale} is likewise borne out numerically:
the phosphate MSD is sub-diffusive with a falling exponent for both g-JF and the
overdamped reference, and the rotational-autocorrelation curve-match to all-atom
DOPC does not overlay under any single time scaling. The membrane clock is thus
demonstrably effective, and the temperature trend demonstrably Arrhenius.
\textbf{[VALIDATED (statics); EFFECTIVE (dynamics)]}

\subsection{The raw force field confines the protein}
\label{sec:hybrid}

Whether the dry-MARTINI field alone holds a protein embedded is a force-field
question, not an integrator one. A rigid 1RKL (internal geometry frozen, free to
translate and rotate) under the raw field stays embedded, settling a few
{\aa}ngstr\"om and plateauing while the surrounding bilayer keeps its thickness; a
flexible 1RKL likewise stays folded (backbone radius of gyration constant) and
embedded, with no sustained drift. \textbf{[VALIDATED]} Replacing the raw BB-env
with a smoothed PMF instead drives the protein out. The earlier ``impulsive kick''
that had motivated softening was traced to under-resolved lipids (a too-small
sub-step count firing a safeguard displacement cap and injecting energy) compounded
by a non-standard reduced timestep; at the standard step with the g-JF integrator
the raw field needs no help, and softening only degrades the confinement it was
meant to protect.

\subsection{Application: hydrogen-deuterium exchange}
\label{sec:hdx}

The motivating application is HDX-MS, which probes the local opening (``breaking'')
and re-forming of backbone hydrogen bonds---the helix stability that reports on
folding and on the membrane's protective effect. This is exactly the kind of
observable the model is built for, because HDX measures a \emph{thermodynamic}
quantity. In the common EX2 regime the exchange rate factorizes,
\begin{equation}
k_\mathrm{HDX} = \frac{k_\mathrm{op}}{k_\mathrm{cl}}\,k_\mathrm{chem}
= K_\mathrm{op}\,k_\mathrm{chem} = e^{-\Delta G_\mathrm{op}/k_BT}\,k_\mathrm{chem},
\end{equation}
so the protection factor and $\Delta G_\mathrm{op}$ depend on the \emph{equilibrium
opening constant} $K_\mathrm{op}$---the fraction of time each amide is open---and
the individual opening and closing \emph{rates} cancel into their ratio. The
effective, non-physical kinetics of the coupled model therefore never enter an EX2
protection factor: what is required is the correct equilibrium open probability,
which the Boltzmann-correct sampling provides. The analysis pipeline computes
residue $\Delta G$ and uptake from ensemble-averaged (MBAR-reweighted) per-frame
protection states, i.e.\ from equilibrium populations, consistent with this.

Four conditions attach. (1) The learned protein force field must give the correct
local helical stability, and the membrane its correct modulation---the former is
Upside's strength, the latter enters thermodynamically. (2) The protein must be held
at the correct \emph{temperature}: because $K_\mathrm{op}=e^{-\Delta G_\mathrm{op}/
k_BT}$ is exponentially sensitive to $T$, an inadequately thermostatted protein
biases every protection factor. In the coupled system this requires the protein to
carry its own Langevin dissipation (Sec.~\ref{sec:units}); the frictionless
velocity-Verlet protein overheats to $k_BT\!\approx\!1.5$--$2$ and grossly inflates
$K_\mathrm{op}$ (spurious over-exchange), whereas at the protein friction that holds
$k_BT\!=\!0.86$ the H-bond populations are stable and still fluctuating (opening is
sampled). The friction is Boltzmann-correct, so once $T$ is matched it does not
otherwise bias $K_\mathrm{op}$. (3) The rare open states must be \emph{converged},
which is why replica exchange and metadynamics are integral rather than optional; the
slow lipid dynamics lengthens convergence but does not change the converged $\Delta
G$. (4) A membrane-specific point: in a dry
(waterless) membrane an amide can be hydrogen-bond-broken yet still buried in the
lipid core and thus not exchange-competent, so the protection criterion must be
``open \emph{and} water-accessible'' (approximating accessibility from bilayer
depth), not open alone; otherwise transmembrane segments are scored as exchanging
when the membrane in fact protects them. With these, membrane-protein HDX sits
squarely in the model's rigorous domain. \textbf{[OPEN, in progress]}

\subsection{Transmembrane helix tilt: a coupled-kinetics limit}
\label{sec:tilt}

In the hybrid at the standard step, 1RKL stays folded and embedded but its backbone
tilt remains near its $\sim$$40^\circ$ insertion value with only
$\pm0.2$--$0.4^\circ$ fluctuation, versus $\pm5$--$6^\circ$ in all-atom and
wet-MARTINI references that relax to $\sim$$10$--$22^\circ$. Because the same lipid
chemistry (wet MARTINI) does relax the tilt, the frozen hybrid tilt is a
\emph{kinetic} failure to relax---annular lipids rearranging too slowly---not a
force-field equilibrium at $40^\circ$. It is the sharpest instance of the
coupled-kinetics limitation, and the correct remedy is enhanced sampling of the
tilt coordinate (metadynamics), not a force-field change. \textbf{[OPEN]}

\section{Discussion}

The model composes a data-rich machine-learned protein force field with a
transferable physical membrane, coupled through raw interactions, so that
membrane-protein thermodynamics can be studied without membrane-protein training
data. Its scope follows directly from how it is built. Statics are rigorous: the
g-JF integrator and the Monte-Carlo barostat both sample the correct Boltzmann and
isothermal--isobaric ensembles, so bilayer structure and mechanics, and the
protein's conformational and local-stability distributions, are those of the force
field. Dynamics are effective: the coarse lipid is intrinsically sub-diffusive with
a mode-dependent, non-physical clock, and coupled protein--lipid kinetics inherit
this. The honest boundary is therefore between \emph{equilibrium} observables
(trustworthy) and \emph{rates} of lipid-coupled processes (not). HDX protection
factors are equilibrium quantities and lie inside the boundary; a helix-tilt
relaxation \emph{rate} lies outside it. Relative statements across temperature or
condition remain reliable because the temperature trend is Arrhenius even though
the absolute rate is not.

Two methodological lessons generalize. First, enhanced sampling is not an add-on
here but a requirement: it both matches the equilibrium target of the tool and
supplies the ergodicity that the slow intrinsic dynamics cannot. Second, before
adding any corrective term one must rule out setup artifacts---timestep and
integrator resolution above all; the softened BB-env ``fixed'' a problem that was
really an under-resolved lipid at a non-standard step, and the fix was itself the
source of the drift.

\section{Outlook}

The principle---compose individually well-determined components through their
physical coupling, rather than train a bespoke potential for the combined
system---is not specific to lipids. Any system in which the parts are parameterized
but the \emph{combination} is data-poor is a candidate: a learned protein or
polymer model together with a physical model of an inorganic or engineered phase,
coupled by their raw cross-interactions. Nanoparticles are a concrete example---a
protein or membrane interacting with a nanoparticle whose surface chemistry is
physically describable but for which little protein-binding data exists. In every
such case the same scope applies: the composite model is a thermodynamic and
conformational-sampling instrument, rigorous for equilibrium observables and
explicit about its effective dynamics.

\section{Conclusions}

A membrane protein and a full-resolution dry-MARTINI bilayer can be propagated
together at Upside's standard step by advancing the lipids with a single
Boltzmann-correct g-JF Langevin step and coupling the protein through the raw force
field, so that the machine-learned protein model and the physical membrane model
combine without any membrane-protein training data. The model's rigorous domain is
thermodynamics: validated bilayer structure and mechanics, and---via enhanced
sampling---protein conformational ensembles and local stabilities, with HDX
protection factors as the flagship equilibrium observable. Its lipid and
coupled-protein dynamics are effective, not physical, which we demonstrate
(sub-diffusive lipid, mode-dependent clock) and which is the correct trade for
those equilibrium targets. The same compose-by-physical-coupling strategy extends
the reach of the model to other data-sparse systems such as nanoparticles.
